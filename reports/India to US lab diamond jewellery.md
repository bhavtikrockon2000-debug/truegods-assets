# Sell Gold and Design, Not Carats

An India-based D2C brand selling 10K/14K gold jewellery set with lab-grown diamonds (LGD) or moissanite to US consumers is **viable, but only as a design-led, gold-cost-disciplined brand with thin early margins. It does not work as a stone-price arbitrage play.** Demand is real and growing. Lab-grown stones were **61% of US engagement rings in 2025**, about **42% of US diamond jewellery**, and are spreading fast into everyday fashion pieces. The US jewellery market grew **5.6% in 2025 and about 9% in H1 2026**. Two things work against a new entrant. First, LGD wholesale prices have fallen about 90% since 2018, so the stone is now a cheap commodity, and gold (about **$4,250/oz** in late September 2026) is the largest cost in most pieces. Second, the landed cost from India now includes about **15.5-16% US duty** (5.5% MFN plus a 10% Section 301 tariff since 24 July 2026), and there is **no de minimis exemption**. India's own **15% gold import duty** (since 13 May 2026) makes duty-free gold sourcing essential. With a realistic $150-300 CAC on a $400-1,500 order, first orders roughly break even, and profit depends on repeat and organic customers. India's advantages are cheap Surat LGD supply, low making charges, and a **2.5-point tariff edge over China, Hong Kong, Thailand and the UAE**. The recommended entry is phased. Start with Etsy plus a Shopify store, shipped DDP by express courier. Then set up a US entity that acts as importer of record and fulfils from a US jewellery 3PL, which unlocks Shopify Payments, BNPL and US returns. Moissanite should be an entry-price line, not the core.

## Lab-grown has won US bridal, and growth has moved to fashion volume

The size of the US jewellery market depends on the definition. Estimates run from about **$63B** (Statista, 2025) and **$78.9B** (IMARC, 2025) to **$106B** in personal spending on jewellery and watches (FRED, 2025) ([IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/united-states-jewelry-market); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/topics/5841/jewelry-market-in-the-united-states/); [Yahoo/ResearchAndMarkets](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/united-states-jewelry-market-report-154400402.html)). Whichever figure you use, the market is growing. Tenoris (Edahn Golan) measured US jewellery sales up **5.6% in 2025** and **9% in H1 2026** ([Tenoris 2025](https://www.tenoris.bi/the-us-jewelry-market-in-2025-with-5-6-sales-growth/); [Tenoris H1 2026](https://www.tenoris.bi/us-jewelry-sales-continue-to-grow-in-h1-2026/)).

Lab-grown stones are the fastest-growing part of that market. The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study, covering more than 10,000 couples, found that **61% of 2025 engagement rings had a lab-grown centre stone**, up 239% since 2020. The average lab-grown ring was 1.9ct at $4,600, against 1.6ct at $7,000 for natural ([Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260218045442/en/The-Knot-Worldwide-Unveils-2026-Real-Weddings-Study)). Tenoris puts lab-grown at **42.1% of all US diamond jewellery sold in 2025**, though the snippet does not say whether that is by units or value. It also reports that H1 2026 growth in LGD jewellery came almost entirely from **more units, with flat average spend** ([Tenoris](https://www.tenoris.bi/us-jewelry-sales-continue-to-grow-in-h1-2026/)). Signet, America's largest jewellery retailer, says LGD is about **40% of bridal-band sales and about 15% of fashion revenue**, and the fashion share doubled in a year ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/signet-shines-lab-grown-diamonds-175000939.html)). Brilliant Earth's fine-jewellery bookings rose **32% in Q2 2026**, reaching 18% of the total, and pieces above $500 grew more than 40% ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/brilliant-earth-group-inc-brlt-231300136.html)). Buyers are younger and increasingly buy for themselves. In a BriteCo survey, three in four Gen Z adults said they would rather buy fine jewellery for themselves than wait for a gift ([Eastern Progress/BriteCo](https://www.easternprogress.com/americans-are-embracing-self-gifting-fine-jewelry/article_02233b47-aff8-5be0-8174-494d4f97f686.html)). This is the opening for everyday rings, studs, pendants and chains.

The catch is price deflation. LGD wholesale prices fell **26% in 2025**, then **14% and 13% year on year in Q1 and Q2 2026**. The decline is slowing, and prices are now near production cost ([Edahn Golan](https://www.edahngolan.com/lab-grown-diamond-wholesale-prices-2025/); [JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/lab-grown-wholesale-freefall/)). De Beers closed Lightbox in 2025, after wholesale LGD jewellery prices fell about **90% from its $800/ct launch price** ([De Beers Group](https://www.debeersgroup.com/news-insights/latest-group-news/2025/de-beers-group-announces-intention-to-close-lightbox-business)). India's average LGD export price fell from **$389/ct in 2022 to $71/ct in January-May 2026** ([Business Standard](https://businessstandard.substack.com/p/indias-diamond-exports-lose-lustre)). Retail has held up better. Paul Zimnisky finds retailers still take **markups of more than 80% over wholesale** on lab-grown, barely changed from 2025 ([JCK/Zimnisky](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/paul-zimnisky-on-diamond-market/)). In practice, the value of a lab-grown piece now comes from its gold, design and brand, not from the stone. A brand should never claim LGD holds its value.

Gold is now the main cost. It hit a record **$5,595/oz on 29 January 2026** and stood at **$4,251 on 24 September 2026**, down about 8.7% over the month but still about 13% higher than a year earlier ([Forever Rox](https://foreverrox.com/blogs/rox-reports/gold-4350-record-high-what-it-means-for-your-jewelry); [Fortune](https://fortune.com/article/current-price-of-gold-09-03-2026/); [Trading Economics](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/gold)). At about $80/g for 14K, a 2 g pendant contains roughly $160 of metal at spot before any labour or stones. Brilliant Earth blamed "high metal costs and tariffs" for its gross-margin dip ([Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/brilliant-earth-group-inc-brlt-231300136.html)). Charles & Colvard, the company that created the moissanite category, filed for **Chapter 11 in March 2026**. It cited falling LGD and moissanite prices and "skyrocketing" metal costs ([National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/14761-charles-colvard-files-for-bankruptcy-citing-price-pressures)), and its assets sold for just $2.7M ([grown-diamonds.com](https://grown-diamonds.com/what-happened-to-charles-and-colvard-ajs-creations-acquisition/)). Moissanite's selling point was being much cheaper than diamond, and cheap LGD has largely removed that gap. No reliable US moissanite market size exists; global estimates range from $0.16B to $1.7B ([Knowledge Sourcing](https://www.knowledge-sourcing.com/report/moissanite-market); [Verified Market Reports](https://www.verifiedmarketreports.com/product/moissanite-market/)). Moissanite therefore works as an entry-price line, not as the core of the brand.

## The open space sits between Mejuri and Brilliant Earth, and Indian competitors already crowd Etsy

US competitors fall into two groups. Bridal-led lab-grown e-tailers are moving down into fine jewellery. Brilliant Earth had **$437.5M in FY2025 sales** and an average order of about $2,000 ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1866757/000162828026014992/brlt-4q25earningsrelease.htm)). Grown Brilliance, now owning Clean Origin, runs 14 stores ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/grown-brilliance-clean-origin/)). Fashion-led D2C brands sell smaller gold-and-LGD pieces. Ana Luisa's solid-gold LGD pieces run from about $185 to $745 ([Ana Luisa](https://www.analuisa.com/collections/lab-grown-diamonds/)). Aurate's LGD range starts with a $448 ring ([Aurate](https://auratenewyork.com/collections/lab-grown-jewelry)). Mejuri, with an estimated $160-178M in e-commerce revenue and an average order of about $225-250, gets about **half its revenue from repeat customers** ([ECDB](https://ecdb.com/resources/sample-data/retailer/mejuri); [FemFounded](https://femfounded.org/case-studies/mejuri/)). Scale alone does not win. Pandora's LGD line was only about 1% of group revenue in 2024, and it dropped its DKK 1B LGD target ([JCK](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/pandora-strong-results-lab-grown/)).

The best opening for an India-made brand is the **$300-1,500 band**. That is above the gold-led, small-stone pieces from Mejuri and Ana Luisa, and below Brilliant Earth's bridal-halo pricing. In this band, Indian setting labour and cheap Surat stones can deliver visibly more stone per dollar: larger studs, tennis-style pieces, statement pendants and 14K solitaires. The competition from India is not the big domestic brands. Limelight, with 75+ stores and ₹275 crore raised, is focused on India ([Entrepreneur India](https://india.entrepreneur.com/business-news/limelight-diamonds-raises-inr-275-crore)), and Solitario expands abroad through franchises ([Franchise India](https://www.franchiseindia.com/insights/en/article/beyond-the-rough-how-indias-lab-grown-diamond-boom-is-turning-into-a-franchising-gold-rush.59717)). The real competition is hundreds of Surat and Jaipur Etsy sellers. One example is Diamondrensu, which reports **13,000+ sales and 5,000+ reviews** since moving to Etsy moissanite and LGD in 2018 ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/in-en/shop/DiamondrensuLabgrown)); another is the whole "Indian moissanite" category ([Etsy](https://www.etsy.com/market/indian_moissanite)). Competing on price in that crowd is a losing game. A new entrant needs original design, a clear brand, and trust signals that US buyers recognise.

India's supply position is strong. It held **35.7% of global LGD exports in 2025**, and the US share of India's LGD shipments fell from 63% in 2022 to **17% in January-May 2026** as trade shifted to Hong Kong ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/industry/news/india-s-natural-diamond-exports-decline-amid-rise-of-lab-grown-gems-126081400834_1.html)). Polished LGD export value fell 10.55% to $1.13B in FY2025-26 even as volumes rose ([GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/news_detail.php?news=india-s-gem-jewellery-exports-in-fy-2025-26-at-usd-27-72-billion-signals-structural-reset-towards-broad-based-growth-reducing-dependence-on-a-single-market)). For a finished-goods brand, that means plentiful, cheap stones on its doorstep. Moissanite is different: India imports it mainly from China under HS 7104 ([Volza](https://www.volza.com/p/moissanite-loose/import/import-in-india/)).

## A 15.5% duty and no de minimis now sit on every parcel

### The current rate is 10% Section 301 plus 5.5% MFN

Gold jewellery set with LGD or moissanite is classified under **HTSUS 7113.19**, which carries **5.5% MFN duty**. India gets no preferential rate, having lost GSP in 2019 ([USITC HTS](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=711319); [ustariffrates.com](https://ustariffrates.com/chapter/jewelry)). The tariff layer on top has changed five times in 14 months.

| Period | Extra tariff on Indian jewellery (on top of 5.5% MFN) | Legal basis | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aug 27, 2025 - Feb 2026 | 50% (25% reciprocal + 25% Russia-oil) | IEEPA | Superseded |
| Feb 6-7, 2026 | 18% (interim-deal framework) | IEEPA | Voided when SCOTUS struck down IEEPA tariffs on Feb 20, 2026 |
| Feb 24 - Jul 24, 2026 | 10% global surcharge | Section 122 | Expired |
| **Jul 24, 2026 onward** | **10% (India in lower tier)** | **Section 301 (forced labour)** | **Current, no automatic expiry** |

Sources: [Baker McKenzie](https://sanctionsnews.bakermckenzie.com/cbp-effectuates-executive-order-increasing-tariffs-by-25-for-a-total-50-on-indian-imports-over-purchases-of-russian-oil/); [CRS on *Learning Resources v. Trump*](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11398); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/trump-administration-imposes-10-section-122-tariff-plan-replace-ieepa-tariffs); [USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/july/ustr-takes-action-forced-labor-section-301-investigations); [Federal Register 2026-15181](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/28/2026-15181/notice-of-actions-in-section-301-investigations-of-acts-policies-and-practices-of-various-economies).

GJEPC, Rapaport and CBP guidance all place India in the **10% tier**. India qualified by adopting a forced-labour import ban. China, Hong Kong, Thailand, Türkiye, the UAE, Israel and Vietnam pay **12.5%** ([GJEPC Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/us-retains-10-tariff-on-indian-exports-under-new-section-301-regime/); [CBP CSMS #69326983](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/421d887); [Rapaport](https://rapaport.com/news/new-us-tariffs-include-diamonds-and-gemstones/)). The 10% covers Indian jewellery, lab-grown and natural diamonds, and coloured gems. There is no carve-out for India, so **jewellery pays about 15.5-16% in total** ([GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/new-u-s-tariff-regime-puts-indias-gem-jewellery-exports-under-pressure-gjepc/)). Some secondary sources report India in the 12.5% tier ([UStariffcalc](https://ustariffcalc.com/section-122-expired-what-replaces-it.html)). The Federal Register notice and the Indian trade body do not support that, so **15.5-16% is the best-supported planning figure**. Model a 12.5% case as a stress test. The 18% "interim deal" rate that still appears in some coverage is legally dead.

The outlook can move either way. Piyush Goyal said on 25 September 2026 that the India-US deal is "almost done and dusted" but depends on India getting a preferential rate. He meets USTR Greer on 30 September-1 October ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/economy/story/india-us-trade-deal-almost-done-and-dusted-says-piyush-goyal-557724-2026-09-25); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-09-04/india-says-us-trade-deal-hinges-on-preferential-tariff-rate)). A signed deal could cut or remove the 10%. On the other side, an open "industrial overcapacity" Section 301 case names India and could add new tariffs ([CRS](https://www.congress.gov/crs-product/LSB11460)). One opportunity is easy to miss: importers of record who paid IEEPA duties in 2025-26 may be owed refunds through CBP's CAPE process ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/ieepa-tariff-refund-update-government-appeals)).

### Every parcel now needs a customs entry

The $800 de minimis exemption was suspended for all countries on **29 August 2025**. In June 2026 CBP made the suspension **indefinite**: from 24 June 2026 for courier and freight, and from 24 July 2026 for mail. It also created a new postal informal-entry process for goods of **$2,500 or less**, with certain exclusions enforced from **22 October 2026** ([FR 2026-12670](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other); [FR 2026-12669](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry)). Shipments above $2,500 need a formal entry with a bond and a licensed broker. In practice, **DDP (seller pays duty, built into the price) is the only acceptable customer experience**. A US buyer asked to pay a duty bill on delivery of a gift is a refused parcel.

### US compliance: disclosure, stamping and origin

The FTC Jewelry Guides require a clear qualifier immediately before the word "diamond" for any lab-grown stone: "laboratory-grown", "laboratory-created", "[manufacturer]-created" or "synthetic". Moissanite must never be presented as diamond ([eCFR 16 CFR 23](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-23); [National Jeweler](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/956-a-quick-review-of-the-ftc-s-rules-on-lab-grown-diamonds)). The FTC has warned sellers that this applies on Instagram and to influencers ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/05/many-facets-advertising-diamonds-clarity)). Under the National Gold and Silver Stamping Act, a gold item stamped "14K" may be no more than 3/1000 below that fineness. **Any quality mark must sit next to a registered or applied-for US trademark** ([LII 15 USC 297](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/297)), and competitors can sue over violations ([Florida Bar](https://flabizlaw.org/member-articles/all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-private-cause-of-action-for-violation-of-the-national-stamping-act/)). India's BIS hallmarking is not required for exports and does not replace the US trademark requirement ([GJEPC](https://www.gjepc.org/news_detail.php?news=gold-jewellery-exports-gold-articles-exempted-from-mandatory-hallmarking-1)). California caps lead at 200 ppm and cadmium at 300 ppm in adult jewellery ([SGS](https://newsletter.sgs.com/eNewsletterPro/uploadedimages/000006/SGS-Safeguards-06511-Prop-65-jewellery-v2-EN-11.pdf)). Anything aimed at children triggers CPSIA testing ([CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Lead/Total-Lead-Content)). Pieces must be marked "India" as country of origin. Remote sellers owe state sales tax once they pass each state's economic-nexus threshold, typically **$100,000**. Marketplaces collect it on the seller's behalf ([Avalara](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/learn/guides/state-by-state-guide-economic-nexus-laws.html)).

## India's 15% gold duty makes duty-free sourcing the whole game

The biggest Indian policy change happened on **13 May 2026**, when effective duty on gold imports rose from **6% to 15%** (10% basic customs duty plus 5% AIDC) to support the rupee ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/india-hikes-bullion-import-duties-to-arrest-rupee-slide.html); [World Gold Council](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/05/india-gold-market-update-import-tightening)). The next day, DGFT **capped gold imported under Advance Authorisation at 100 kg** and required a physical factory inspection for first-time applicants ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/big-change-in-gold-import-rules-after-15-duty-hike-govt-caps-duty-free-imports-at-100-kg-531593-2026-05-14)). An exporter that buys duty-paid gold on the domestic market starts with a cost handicap of roughly 15%. There are three competitive routes. One is gold from a nominated agency under the replenishment scheme ([DGFT FTP Ch. 4](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/a11b458e-0d61-460f-aef4-b201ed40a9aa/FTP2023_Chapter04.pdf)). Another is the brand's own Advance Authorisation, subject to the new cap and inspection. The simplest for a brand without a factory is contract manufacturing through a unit in SEEPZ-SEZ (Mumbai) or a Surat gems-and-jewellery SEZ, where gold enters duty-free. All three routes impose minimum value-addition and wastage norms. One secondary source cites **6-7% minimum value addition and 5% maximum wastage for studded jewellery**, but these figures need checking against the current Handbook of Procedures ([taxguru](https://taxguru.in/dgft/value-addition-norms-avail-duty-exemption-gem-jewellery.html)). Retail margins clear those thresholds easily. The real constraint is paperwork.

The registration stack is standard: IEC, GST registration with a Letter of Undertaking (LUT) so exports go out without IGST, AD code, and GJEPC membership (RCMC). Parcel-by-parcel e-commerce exports of jewellery follow CBIC's courier jewellery SOP, **Circular 09/2022** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/07/05/regulation-of-provisions-on-re-import-of-jewellery-exported-on-e-commerce-platforms-vide-courier-imports-and-exports-electronic-declaration-and-processing-amendment-regulations-2022/)). Two 2026 changes matter here. From **1 April 2026, CBIC removed the ₹10 lakh per-consignment cap on courier exports**, so high-value pieces and consolidated shipments to a US 3PL can go by courier. But CBIC **excluded jewellery from the new risk-based re-import framework for returns**, so returned pieces still face the stricter identity checks ([TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=16128); [GJEPC](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/govt-removes-%E2%82%B910-lakh-cap-on-courier-export-gjepc-applauds-courier-reforms-but-urges-extension-of-risk-based-norms-to-jewellery/)). Export incentives are minor. RoDTEP was extended only to **30 September 2026**, and its rates for gems and jewellery are small because raw materials dominate cost ([Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/rodtep-scheme-in-2026-meaning-rates-claim-process-practical-guide-for-exporters)). The new **FEMA (Export and Import) Regulations, 2026 take effect 1 October 2026** and keep a 15-month deadline for realising export proceeds ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/realisation-repatriation-export-proceeds-rules/)).

Manufacturing is where India wins. Retail-quoted making charges run about **₹400-800/g for 18K**, or **8-14% of gold value for machine-cast pendants and 15-22% for complex studded pieces** ([Jewelove](https://www.jewelove.in/blogs/news/gold-making-charges); [BlueStone](https://blog.bluestone.com/making-charges-on-gold-2026/)). B2B export job-work rates should be lower. Surat has a dense base of private-label LGD jewellery manufacturers ([IndiaMART](https://dir.indiamart.com/surat/lab-grown-diamond.html)). No reliable B2B job-work or IGI grading fee schedule was found, so both need direct quotes.

## Logistics and payments push founders toward a US entity sooner than expected

For single orders, express courier with DDP is the default route. Aggregator rates for FedEx, DHL and UPS run about **$62-75/kg with 2-6 day transit** ([ClickPost](https://www.clickpost.ai/blog/courier-charges-for-usa)), and Shiprocket X sells a US DDP product ([Shiprocket](https://www.shiprocket.in/blog/delivery-duty-paid-ddp-usa-shipments/)). The weak point is insurance. Carrier liability for jewellery is capped at **$500 per package (UPS) or $1,000 (FedEx standard)**. FedEx's $25,000 international Declared Value Advantage is contract-only and aimed at US accounts ([Shippo](https://goshippo.com/blog/how-to-ship-jewelry-for-your-e-commerce-store-tips-examples-for-shipping-jewelry); [FedEx](https://www.fedex.com/en-us/shipping/high-value-jewelry-declared-value-form.html)). Real cover comes from specialists. Parcel Pro (owned by UPS) covers up to $100K internationally ([Parcel Pro](https://www.parcelpro.com/us/en/insured-shipping.html)). Malca-Amit offers Lloyd's-backed full liability from its India operation to its New York division ([Malca-Amit](https://www.malca-amit.com/diamonds-jewellery/secured-delivery)). India Post resumed US mail with a DDP product on **15 October 2025** ([NewsOnAir](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/india-resumes-international-postal-services-to-us-after-suspension)). How postal duty is calculated after July 2026 is unclear, so post is not yet a dependable channel for fine jewellery.

Payments are the main reason to set up a US entity. **Shopify Payments is not available to Indian entities** ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/setting-up-international-card-payments-on-shopify-india-a-step-by-step-razorpay-integration-guide-2026/)). An India-only seller must use Razorpay at about 3% plus GST ([Razorpay](https://razorpay.com/blog/shopify-payment-gateway-cost-india/)), PayPal at **4.4% + $0.30 plus a 3-4% FX markup** ([PayPal IN](https://www.paypal.com/in/business/paypal-business-fees)), or invite-only Stripe India. On top of that, Shopify charges a **2% third-party-gateway surcharge on the Basic plan** ([Swell](https://www.swell.is/content/shopify-pricing)). The bigger loss is financing at checkout. Shop Pay Installments is limited to US, Canadian and UK merchants ([Shopify](https://www.shopify.com/blog/buy-now-pay-later)), and Klarna needs a local entity ([Klarna](https://www.klarna.com/international/enterprise/platforms-and-partners/shopify/)). Losing BNPL on $1,000+ tickets costs real conversions. Fraud is severe in this category. Signifyd finds fraud rates on orders over $500 run **22 times** those on orders under $100 ([Signifyd](https://www.signifyd.com/blog/ecommerce-fraud-eight-industries/)). Guaranteed fraud protection is therefore a necessity, not an option.

A US entity costs little: about **$500 through Stripe Atlas for a Delaware LLC, or $100-150 for a DIY Wyoming LLC** ([Tax Haven Directory](https://taxhavendirectory.com/blog/stripe-atlas-vs-us-llc-non-residents)). It can act as importer of record, bring in stock at an arm's-length transfer price, pay duty once per bulk entry, and fulfil from a specialist jewellery 3PL such as Buske or Speed Commerce ([Buske](https://www.buske.com/industries-served/jewelry-products)). It also unlocks Shopify Payments and BNPL. The trade-offs are US tax exposure once it holds inventory, FEMA overseas direct investment (ODI) filings, and transfer-pricing documentation. Returns make the case stronger. Online jewellery returns run **12-20%** ([Corso](https://www.corso.com/post-purchase-resource-center/ecommerce-return-rate-benchmarks-by-industry)), and duty paid on a DDP parcel is normally lost when the item comes back. Re-importing into India is duty-free under Notification 45/2017, but only for the identical goods, sent back by the same consignee through the same platform ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/regulatory-framework-e-commerce-exports-jewellery-courier-mode.html)). A US returns address fixes the cost problem and also reassures buyers.

## A 1ct solitaire lands at $515-740; first orders roughly break even

The deductions from there are steep. Payments take 5-8% through an Indian gateway plus the Shopify surcharge, or about 3-4% through a US entity. The returns reserve is 12-20% of orders. Customer acquisition is the largest cost. The duty scenario matters less than it looks. Each 2.5 points of tariff adds only about $11-14 to this ring. A 10-point swing, such as a deal cutting the 301 tariff to zero, moves landed cost by about $45-55. CAC, gold weight and return rate move profit far more than the tariff does.

## Five risks, and a three-phase entry that hedges them

**Tariff volatility** is the most visible risk. The rate has changed five times since August 2025, and it could fall with a deal or rise under the overcapacity case. The hedge is pricing with a DDP buffer and keeping a US entity ready so the brand can switch between parcel and bulk import. **Gold price swings** of 20% in eight months can wipe out a fixed-price catalogue. The hedge is light and hollow designs, 10K entry price points, and repricing linked to the gold rate, as Indian jewellers already do. **LGD deflation** erodes the brand's value story and any inventory held. The hedge is to sell design, never resale value, and keep stone inventory lean. **Fraud, chargebacks and returns** are unusually heavy for high-ticket jewellery shipped across borders, and India's own customs rules make returns harder. The hedge is guaranteed fraud screening, laser inscriptions matched to certificates, and a US returns address. **Commoditisation**, from hundreds of Indian Etsy sellers competing on moissanite price, is the risk most likely to quietly kill a new brand. The hedge is original, trademarked design and an owned customer list. Compliance risks are FTC disclosure, the stamping trademark and Prop 65. They are manageable but must be built in from day one. A false "14K" stamp or an unqualified "diamond" in an ad is a lawsuit waiting to happen.

The recommended entry runs in three phases. **Phase 1, months 0-4 (set-up):**
1. Incorporate in India and obtain IEC, GST with LUT, AD code and GJEPC RCMC.
2. Contract a SEEPZ or Surat SEZ manufacturer (or a nominated-agency gold arrangement) so gold is duty-free.
3. File a US trademark for the stamping requirement.
4. Build 20-40 original SKUs in 14K, with 10K entry pieces. Focus on studs, pendants, chains, stackable rings and 1ct solitaires at **$250-1,500**, with a moissanite line below $300.

**Phase 2, months 4-12 (validation):** sell on **Etsy** for traffic and reviews, alongside a **Shopify store** using Razorpay or PayPal. Ship every order DDP by express courier with third-party insurance. Use a US returns consolidator from the start. **Phase 3, from roughly $250-500k in annualised US revenue or the first sign of tariff clarity:**
1. Form a US LLC or C-corp to act as importer of record.
2. Move bestsellers into a jewellery 3PL, and keep made-to-order and high-value pieces on direct DDP.
3. Switch on Shopify Payments, Shop Pay Installments and Signifyd.
4. Scale Google Shopping and Meta, then apply for Amazon Fine Jewelry and TikTok Shop in year two.

Near-term dates to watch: the Goyal-Greer meeting (30 September-1 October), RoDTEP's expiry (30 September), the new FEMA regulations (1 October), and enforcement of the postal-entry exclusions (22 October).

## Conclusion

The usual pitch for this business is that Indian stones are cheap and US diamond prices are high. That stopped working when LGD prices collapsed to near cost. What is left is a manufacturing and brand opportunity. India can turn cheap stones and skilled setting labour into well-designed 14K pieces at a landed cost that supports $300-1,500 retail. It also enjoys a small tariff advantage over its closest Asian rivals, precisely because the US now taxes everyone. Gold price, CAC and return rate decide profitability far more than the tariff does. Founders who spend months waiting for the trade deal are optimising the wrong variable.

The other conclusion is structural. A pure India-to-US parcel model is fine for testing demand. It is a poor way to scale, because it gives up BNPL, pays duty that is lost on every return, and faces stricter re-import rules for jewellery than for other goods. The businesses that last will probably look like two companies: an Indian manufacturer-exporter sourcing duty-free gold in an SEZ, and a US brand entity that owns the customer, the checkout and the returns. The open questions are the Section 301 annex treatment of 7113.19, the post-July postal duty basis, and true B2B making and grading costs. All three should be confirmed with a customs broker and supplier quotes before committing inventory.
