# US Import Regulations, Tariffs and Compliance: Gold Jewellery with Lab-Grown Diamonds / Moissanite Shipped from India (status as of 2026-09-25)

Research note: primary government pages (federalregister.gov, ecfr.gov, hts.usitc.gov, gjepc.org, law-firm sites) were blocked by the network proxy for direct fetching in this session, so the findings below come from search-result extracts of those pages plus secondary coverage. Treat exact numeric details (especially HTS stat suffixes and loose-stone duty rates) as needing a final check against hts.usitc.gov before you rely on them.

## 1. HTS classification and MFN (base) duty rates

### Takeaway
Gold jewellery set with lab-grown diamonds (LGD) or moissanite goes under HTSUS 7113.19 (jewellery of precious metal other than silver). The MFN rate is 5.5% for the main "other" subheadings (7113.19.29, 7113.19.50). India gets no preference: GSP for India ended in 2019 and is not listed in the "Special" column. Loose LGD go under heading 7104 (synthetic stones), not 7102 (natural diamonds).

### Cited Findings
- HTSUS 7113.19.50 (precious-metal jewellery other than silver, n.e.s.o.i.) has a 5.5% MFN rate. — [UNIS HTS 7113.19.50](https://www.unisco.com/hts/71131950); [USITC HTS 7113.19.50.91](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=7113195091)
- HTSUS 7113.19.29.00 is also 5.5% MFN. The Special column lists Free for A*, AU, BH, CL, CO, D, E, IL, JO, KR, MA, OM, P, PA, PE, S, SG. India is not among those FTA or preference partners. — [ustariffrates.com Chapter 71](https://ustariffrates.com/chapter/jewelry); [USITC HTS 7113.19](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=711319)
- GJEPC and trade press put US MFN duty on Indian jewellery at 5.5% to 6%. Before the 2025 tariffs, jewellery paid 5 to 7% and loose polished diamonds/gems paid 0%. — [Whalesbook, 2026](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/commodities/New-US-Tariffs-Raise-Jewellery-Import-Duty-to-16percent-for-India/6a64810ed1570ec59e535399); [Business Standard, 7 Feb 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/industry/news/zero-duty-us-access-for-diamonds-coloured-gems-a-turning-point-ind-inc-126020700883_1.html)
- Synthetic (lab-grown) diamonds are classified in heading 7104, not 7102 (natural). Under HS2022, 7104.21 covers unworked or simply sawn synthetic diamonds and 7104.91 covers other (worked/polished) synthetic diamonds. — [FreightAmigo: HS code for polished synthetic diamonds](https://www.freightamigo.com/en/blog/hs-code/hs-code-for-polished-synthetic-diamonds-for-trade/); [USITC 7104.21.00.00](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=7104.21.00.00); [USITC 7104.91.10.00](https://hts.usitc.gov/search?query=7104.91.10.00)
- One secondary source says "lab grown diamonds will have a duty rate of 3%," contrasting this with duty-free natural loose diamonds. This is unverified; see Gaps. — [JCK: No Duties on Diamonds?](https://www.jckonline.com/editorial-article/duties-diamonds-tariff-questions/)
- The HTSUS current revision seen was "2026 HTS Revision 10 (06/08/2026)." — [htshub](https://www.htshub.com/us-hs/chapter/71-7)

### Inferences
- The set stones do not change the heading. A gold ring with an LGD or moissanite centre stone is still 7113.19 jewellery (the precious metal gives the essential character). The exact 10-digit stat suffix depends on article type (ring, earring, necklace, etc.) and gold content. A broker should confirm it, or you can get a binding ruling via CBP CROSS (rulings.cbp.gov).
- Selling loose LGD separately would put them under 7104.91. Loose moissanite (synthetic silicon carbide) would most likely go under 7104.99 as "other" worked synthetic stones. Do not rely on either without a ruling.

### Gaps
- I could not fetch the exact MFN rates for 7104.91.10 / 7104.91.50 (polished LGD) or 7104.99 (moissanite) from USITC because it was blocked. The 3% figure is secondary and unconfirmed. Historically, worked synthetic stones "suitable for use in jewelry" were Free, but I could not verify this for the 2026 HTS.
- I did not retrieve the complete list of 10-digit stat suffixes under 7113.19.

## 2. US tariffs on Indian goods, 2025 to 2026, and the latest effective rate (Sept 2026)

### Takeaway
As of 25 Sept 2026, gold jewellery from India pays the 5.5% MFN duty plus a 10% Section 301 "forced-labor" tariff, about 15.5 to 16% in total. That 10% also covers loose natural and lab-grown diamonds from India. The 50% IEEPA stack (25% reciprocal plus 25% Russia-oil penalty) from August 2025 is gone. The Russia-oil 25% was revoked on 6 Feb 2026, and all IEEPA tariffs were struck down by the Supreme Court on 20 Feb 2026. They were replaced first by a temporary Section 122 surcharge (24 Feb to 24 Jul 2026) and then, from 24 Jul 2026, by the Section 301 tariff. No bilateral trade agreement had been signed as of 25 Sept 2026.

### Inferences
- **Landed duty today (Sept 2026)** on a USD 1,000 customs value 14k/18k gold LGD ring: MFN 5.5% ($55) plus Section 301 10% ($100) = $155 (15.5%), before the merchandise processing fee and broker or carrier fees. No Section 232 layer appears to apply to finished gold jewellery.
- The Feb 2026 "18% on jewellery" figure is **superseded**. It was an IEEPA rate, void after the SCOTUS ruling. Do not use it in pricing models.
- **Upside risks:** a signed India-US deal could lower or remove the 10%, and India's lobbying specifically targets diamonds and gems. **Downside risks:** the overcapacity Section 301 case, and a change of tier if India's forced-labour enforcement is found inadequate. Section 301 actions carry no statutory expiry, unlike the 150-day limit under Section 122.
- An India-based D2C seller that acted as importer of record in 2025 and paid IEEPA duties, or whose DDP carrier or broker did, may be owed refunds through CAPE. In postal/DDP setups the refund normally goes to the party that paid as importer of record.

### Gaps
- I could not open the Section 301 Annex I/II HTS lists to confirm that 7113.19 and 7104 are not exempted for India. I relied on GJEPC/Rapaport statements that jewellery and LGD are covered.
- The Section 122 rate (10% vs 15%) is inconsistent across sources. It no longer matters for current rates but does affect refund calculations if the CIT ruling against Section 122 is upheld.

## 3. End of de minimis, postal/courier duty collection, DDP vs DDU, entry thresholds, brokers

### Takeaway
The USD 800 de minimis (Section 321) exemption was suspended for all countries from 29 Aug 2025. CBP made the suspension indefinite in June 2026: from 24 Jun 2026 for courier/express/freight and from 24 Jul 2026 for mail. Every D2C parcel from India now owes MFN duty plus the 10% Section 301 duty. Shipments of USD 2,500 or less can use informal entry; above USD 2,500 needs a formal entry with a bond and usually a licensed customs broker. DDP (seller-paid, via a carrier or broker platform) is effectively the only good customer experience.

### Cited Findings
- EO "Suspending Duty-Free De Minimis Treatment for All Countries" (July 2025) suspended the exemption for goods of USD 800 or less from 29 Aug 2025, under IEEPA, the NEA and section 604 of the Trade Act 1974. — [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/07/suspending-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-all-countries/); [CBP fact sheet (updated 18 Aug 2025)](https://www.cbp.gov/sites/default/files/2025-08/factsheet_suspension_of_duty-free_de_minimis_treatment.pdf); [CBP CSMS #66065494](https://content.govdelivery.com/accounts/USDHSCBP/bulletins/3f01456)
- **Superseded 2025 postal method:** Postal carriers could choose a flat "specific duty" per item for 6 months or an ad valorem duty equal to the IEEPA rate for the country of origin. The specific-duty option ended 28 Feb 2026, and only ad valorem has been allowed since. — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_gl/technical/tax-alerts/us-suspends-duty-free-de-minimis-treatment-for-low-value-shipments); [Easyship](https://www.easyship.com/blog/de-minimis-exemption-suspension)

  - (b) 91 FR 37801 (FR Doc 2026-12669) indefinitely suspends de minimis for mail and creates a new postal informal entry process for merchandise of USD 2,500 or less, effective 24 Jul 2026. Enforcement of certain exclusions begins 22 Oct 2026.
  - [FR 2026-12670](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12670/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-merchandise-arriving-through-all-modes-other); [FR 2026-12669](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12669/indefinite-suspension-of-the-de-minimis-exemption-for-mail-shipments-and-new-postal-informal-entry); [Justia summary](https://regulations.justia.com/regulations/fedreg/2026/06/24/2026-12669.html); [Alba](https://albawheelsup.com/trade_news/cbp-makes-it-official-de-minimis-is-gone-for-good-and-a-new-postal-entry-process-is-on-the-way/)

### Inferences
- **Courier (DHL/FedEx/UPS/Aramex) ≤ USD 2,500:** the courier files an informal entry (Type 11) as broker and bills duty plus a brokerage or disbursement fee. With DDU/DAP the US customer is billed on delivery, which drives refusals and returns for a luxury item. With DDP the seller is billed. Recommendation: DDP with duties built into the price, or a DDP platform (Zonos, Global-e, Easyship, etc.).
- **Courier > USD 2,500** (e.g. a higher-value LGD solitaire or a bulk stock shipment): formal entry (Type 01) with a continuous or single-transaction bond and a licensed customs broker. The importer of record needs a CBP IOR number (a foreign IOR can use a CBP-assigned number) and usually a US agent for service.
- **India Post / EMS:** under the new regime, the foreign post or a qualified party must collect and pay duty. Postal delays, bond requirements and the owner/broker filing rules make post less practical for fine jewellery than express courier.
- **Alternative model (bulk import):** ship inventory formally to a US 3PL, pay duty once on wholesale transfer value, and fulfil domestically. This avoids per-parcel entry fees and makes returns easier. Duty is then on the (lower) transfer price, which must be arm's-length under the transaction-value rules.
- The first-sale/transaction value on the commercial invoice drives duty. Under-valuation of jewellery (a common D2C shortcut) risks penalties under 19 USC 1592 (general knowledge, not sourced here).

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve exact current Merchandise Processing Fee amounts for informal and formal entries, or the postal-entry fee structure.
- It is unclear whether 7113 jewellery containing any regulated material triggers PGA exclusion from postal informal entry. Adult gold jewellery is generally not PGA-regulated; children's products (CPSC) may be.

## 4. FTC Jewelry Guides, Stamping Act, Prop 65/state laws, COO marking, sales tax, product liability

### Takeaway
LGD must always carry a clear, immediately preceding qualifier ("laboratory-grown", "laboratory-created", "[manufacturer]-created" or "synthetic"). Moissanite and other simulants must never be called "diamond" or implied to be diamond. Gold must be stamped at the true karat within Stamping Act tolerances, with a registered trademark next to any quality mark. Sales into California must meet the lead/cadmium metal-containing-jewellery limits. Each article must carry the country of origin ("India"). Remote sellers owe state sales tax once they pass each state's economic nexus threshold (usually USD 100,000). Children's jewellery triggers CPSIA testing and certification.

### Cited Findings
**FTC Jewelry Guides (16 CFR Part 23, revised Aug 2018)**
- The Guides say marketers should not use the name of any precious stone (including diamond) for a laboratory-created stone unless it is immediately preceded by a clear and conspicuous disclosure that it is not mined (§23.25). — [eCFR 16 CFR Part 23](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-23); [FTC 2018 Federal Register revision](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/08/16/2018-17454/guides-for-the-jewelry-precious-metals-and-pewter-industries)
- Approved qualifiers are "laboratory-grown", "laboratory-created", "[manufacturer name]-created" or "synthetic", and one of them must precede the gem name whenever the stone is offered for sale. CZ and moissanite are not diamond (natural or lab-grown), and that must be made clear. — [National Jeweler: A Quick Review of the FTC's Rules on Lab-Grown Diamonds](https://nationaljeweler.com/articles/956-a-quick-review-of-the-ftc-s-rules-on-lab-grown-diamonds); [FTC 2018 revision](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2018/08/16/2018-17454/guides-for-the-jewelry-precious-metals-and-pewter-industries)
- **Enforcement:** In April 2019 FTC staff sent warning letters to eight firms advertising simulated or lab-created diamonds without adequate disclosure. The FTC said the Guides and the FTC Act (Section 5, deception) apply on Instagram, Facebook and Twitter, including to influencers. — [FTC Business Blog, May 2019](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/05/many-facets-advertising-diamonds-clarity); [FTC letters to diamond companies (PDF)](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/documents/foia_requests/letters_to_diamond_companies.pdf)
- The Guides are non-binding interpretations, but violations can be pursued as unfair or deceptive practices under Section 5 of the FTC Act. — [ADIF: Diamond labelling rules, are penalties enforced?](https://adiamondisforever.com/about-diamonds/trust-and-regulation-how-diamond-labelling-is-governed/)

**National Gold and Silver Stamping Act (15 U.S.C. §§294–300)**
- The Act regulates quality marks on gold, silver and platinum articles in interstate and foreign commerce, and §294 makes it unlawful to import falsely marked gold or silver articles. — [15 USC Ch. 8 (uscode.house.gov)](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=%2Fprelim%40title15%2Fchapter8&edition=prelim); [LII 15 USC 294](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/294)
- **Tolerance:** for gold articles, the actual fineness may be no more than 3/1000 below the stamped fineness. The Act also provides a wider tolerance for whole-article assays including solder. — [APMEX learning guide](https://learn.apmex.com/learning-guide/the-national-gold-and-silver-stamping-act-of-1906-how-america-standardized-precious-metal-markings/); [15 USC Ch. 8](https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=%2Fprelim%40title15%2Fchapter8&edition=prelim)
- **Trademark requirement (15 USC §297):** anyone who applies a quality mark must also apply a registered US trademark (or an applied-for one) or registered name next to it, so the claim can be traced. — [LII 15 USC 297](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/15/297)
- Competitors and consumers have a private cause of action for violations. — [Florida Bar Business Law Section](https://flabizlaw.org/member-articles/all-that-glitters-is-not-gold-private-cause-of-action-for-violation-of-the-national-stamping-act/)

**Prop 65 / state heavy-metal jewellery laws**
- California Metal-Containing Jewelry Law (Health & Safety Code §25214.1 et seq.) plus Prop 65 settlements set lead at no more than 200 ppm (0.02%) by weight for covered products sold after 31 Aug 2009. Cadmium is limited to 300 ppm in any component for adult jewellery and 75 ppm for children's jewellery. — [SGS SafeGuards: Prop 65 jewellery](https://newsletter.sgs.com/eNewsletterPro/uploadedimages/000006/SGS-Safeguards-06511-Prop-65-jewellery-v2-EN-11.pdf); [OEM Jewelry Lab](https://oemjewelrylab.com/prop-65-jewelry-compliance/)
- Connecticut, Illinois and Minnesota apply 75 ppm cadmium limits for children's jewellery, stricter than California's original proposal. — [CA SB 929 floor analysis](https://www.leginfo.ca.gov/pub/09-10/bill/sen/sb_0901-0950/sb_929_cfa_20100817_175907_asm_floor.html)

**CPSIA (children's jewellery only)**
- Children's products (intended for age 12 or under) are limited to 100 ppm total lead in substrate and 90 ppm in paint or surface coatings. They need third-party testing by a CPSC-accepted lab and a Children's Product Certificate (CPC). ASTM F2923 applies to children's jewellery. — [CPSC Total Lead Content](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Lead/Total-Lead-Content); [QIMA](https://blog.qima.com/lab-testing/childrens-jewelry-cpsia-regulations); [ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/children-jewelry-regulations-united-states/)

**Country-of-origin marking (19 USC 1304 / 19 CFR Part 134)**
- Imported articles must be marked with country of origin in a conspicuous, legible and permanent way, or the container must be marked where the article cannot be. 19 CFR 134.43 has special permanent-marking rules (cutting, die-sinking, engraving, stamping) for Native American-style jewellery. — [ComplianceGate](https://www.compliancegate.com/children-jewelry-regulations-united-states/)

**State sales tax / economic nexus**
- Since *South Dakota v. Wayfair* (2018), 41 states use a USD 100,000 sales threshold, 2 use USD 250,000 and 3 use USD 500,000. About 16 to 17 states have dropped the 200-transaction test (Avalara, updated 3 Aug 2026). All sales-tax states have marketplace-facilitator laws (Amazon, Etsy, etc. collect for you). Foreign sellers protected by treaty from federal income tax can still owe state sales and use tax collection. — [Avalara state guide](https://www.avalara.com/us/en/learn/guides/state-by-state-guide-economic-nexus-laws.html); [Sales Tax Institute](https://www.salestaxinstitute.com/resources/economic-nexus-state-guide); [Thomson Reuters](https://tax.thomsonreuters.com/blog/nexus-considerations-in-a-post-wayfair-world/); [The Tax Adviser](https://www.thetaxadviser.com/issues/2023/jun/south-dakota-v-wayfair-five-years-later/)

### Inferences
- **Copy and listing rules:** use "lab-grown diamond" everywhere, including titles, alt text, social posts, ads and product feeds. Never "diamond" alone, "real diamond" or "genuine diamond" for LGD. Moissanite should be described as "moissanite" or "lab-created moissanite", and marketing it as a "diamond alternative" is fine, but "moissanite diamond" should be avoided. "Cultured diamond" is permitted by the FTC only with an immediate, equally conspicuous disclosure. Stone grading reports (IGI/GIA) for LGD must say lab-grown.
- **Gold marking:** stamp "14K"/"18K" (or 585/750) plus a registered trademark (US registration recommended) on each piece. Indian BIS hallmarking does not replace the US trademark requirement. FTC Guides (§23.4) say not to call anything under 10K "gold", and not to use "solid gold" for hollow items. That section of the Guides was not directly retrieved here.
- **COO:** laser-engrave or stamp "India" on the piece where feasible. Otherwise mark tags and packaging as CBP rules allow. Pieces made from US-origin LGD set in India are still "Product of India".
- **Sales tax:** below thresholds there is no obligation in most states. Selling via Shopify DTC requires tracking per-state revenue. Selling through US marketplaces moves collection to the platform.
- **Product liability / consumer protection** (not directly sourced here; flag for counsel): state UDAP laws, California's Song-Beverly and Consumer Legal Remedies Act, the FTC Mail, Internet or Telephone Order Merchandise Rule (ship within the stated time or within 30 days; notify delays and offer cancellation), FTC Negative Option / "Click-to-Cancel" and fake-review rules, the Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act if written warranties are offered, and nickel-allergy claims for white-gold alloys. A US product liability insurance policy is advisable when using a US IOR or 3PL.
