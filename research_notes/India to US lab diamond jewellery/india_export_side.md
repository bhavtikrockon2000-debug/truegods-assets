## 1. Registrations: IEC, GJEPC RCMC, GST/LUT, AD code, KYC

### Takeaway
The usual Indian exporter stack applies (IEC, then RCMC, then GST registration with a LUT for zero-rated exports, then AD code registration at the port or courier terminal). For courier-mode e-commerce jewellery exports, CBIC's jewellery SOP (Circular 09/2022) adds requirements on top of this. I could not open the primary DGFT/CBIC pages in this session, so the registration mechanics below are mostly unverified.

### Cited Findings
- CBIC Circular No. 09/2022-Customs (30 Jun 2022) issued an SOP with a "simplification and regulation framework for e-commerce exports of jewellery through Courier mode". The Courier Imports and Exports (Electronic Declaration and Processing) Regulations, 2010 were amended by Notification No. 57/2022-Customs (N.T.) of the same date. — [SCC Online, 5 Jul 2022](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/07/05/regulation-of-provisions-on-re-import-of-jewellery-exported-on-e-commerce-platforms-vide-courier-imports-and-exports-electronic-declaration-and-processing-amendment-regulations-2022/); [CBIC Circular 09/2022 PDF](https://courier.cbic.gov.in/advisory/2022/Circular-No-09-2022.pdf) (listed in search results but not readable here); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/cbic-issues-sops-for-ecommerce-exports-of-jewellery-via-couriers/)
- GJEPC says its advocacy produced "clarification of courier mode exports for gems and jewellery after a 22-year period of uncertainty", and got the Dak Ghar Niryat Kendra network introduced and expanded. — [GJEPC Solitaire: GJEPC Driving Growth in E-commerce Exports](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/gjepc-driving-growth-in-indias-gems-jewellery-e-commerce-exports/)
- DGFT publishes a "How to Export" practical guide covering the basic steps. — [DGFT HTE.pdf](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/HTE.pdf) (not readable here)

### Inferences
- **[unverified]** Standard sequence: company or LLP, then PAN and bank current account, then IEC on the DGFT portal (govt fee about INR 500, annual update required), then GST registration, then an annual LUT (Form GST RFD-11) so exports go out without IGST (zero-rated, with ITC refund), then AD code registered with ICEGATE or the courier terminal, then GJEPC membership/RCMC (needed for FTP benefits such as Advance Authorisation and for GJEPC programmes). Also needed: KYC with the authorised courier and ICEGATE, and foreign-exchange realisation via eBRC. The alternative to a LUT is paying IGST on export and claiming a refund, which blocks working capital.
- A D2C founder who ships single orders to US consumers should plan on courier-mode e-commerce export under Circular 09/2022, or postal mode via DNK. Exporting in bulk to a US 3PL or subsidiary is a separate regular-cargo route.

## 2. Gold sourcing for exports: import duty, Advance Authorisation, Replenishment, nominated agencies, SEZs, value addition and wastage

### Takeaway
The biggest 2026 change: on **13 May 2026 India raised the effective duty on gold and silver imports from 6% to 15%** (10% BCD + 5% AIDC). On 14 May 2026 DGFT **capped gold imports under Advance Authorisation at 100 kg** and added stricter checks, including physical inspection for first-time applicants. That makes duty-neutral routes (AA, replenishment via nominated agencies, SEZ/EOU) essential for a price-competitive exporter. Buying duty-paid domestic gold and claiming drawback is much less attractive.

### Cited Findings
- **Duty history:** the July 2024 budget cut duty to 6%. For Budget 2026 (Feb 2026), sources conflict. [Aditya Birla Capital](https://www.adityabirlacapital.com/abc-of-money/budget-2026-impact-on-jewellery-prices) says it was cut to 5%; [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/economy/budget-2026-keeps-gold-import-duty-unchanged-amid-trade-balance-concerns) says it was kept unchanged. Several sources treat the pre-May-2026 rate as 6%, so the "cut to 5%" claim looks doubtful.
- **Hike, 13 May 2026:** "Government raised effective import duty on gold and silver from 6% to 15%". Structure is 10% basic customs duty plus a 5% levy (AIDC), with notifications effective 13 May 2026, amid rupee weakness and CAD concerns. It is described as the steepest increase on record. — [CNBC, 13 May 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/05/13/india-hikes-bullion-import-duties-to-arrest-rupee-slide.html); [Vision IAS, 15 May 2026](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/news-today/2026-05-15/economy/government-raised-effective-import-duty-on-gold-and-silver-from-6-to-15); [MMTC-PAMP blog](https://www.mmtcpamp.com/blog/blog-detail/gold-silver-import-duty-hike); [World Gold Council, May 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/05/india-gold-market-update-import-tightening)
- **AA cap, 14 May 2026:** the government "capped gold imports under the Advance Authorisation (AA) scheme at 100 kg" to prevent misuse. It also added stricter compliance conditions for gold AA applicants, including "mandatory physical inspection of the manufacturing facility for first-time applicants to verify existence, production capacity and operational status". — [Business Today, 14 May 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/big-change-in-gold-import-rules-after-15-duty-hike-govt-caps-duty-free-imports-at-100-kg-531593-2026-05-14); [Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/gold-import-restrictions-india-jewellery-exports-2026/)
- WGC expects combined jewellery and bar/coin demand to fall by about 50-60 t (about 10% YoY) because of the hike. — [WGC via SMM](https://news.metal.com/newscontent/103922055-India-gold-market-update-Import-tightening)
- **FTP 2023 Chapter 4 mechanics:** exports under gem & jewellery schemes are "subject to wastage norms and minimum value addition as prescribed in paragraph 4.60 and 4.61 respectively in the Handbook of Procedures". A Replenishment Authorisation for Gems can be issued against exports made with metal supplied by a Nominated Agency (FTP para 4.41) or by a foreign buyer (para 4.45). For studded gold jewellery, the Gem Replenishment Authorisation value is based on "the remaining FOB value of exports after deducting the value of gold/silver/platinum including admissible wastage". For the replenishment scheme, the value-addition calculation uses the nominated agency's actual purchase price plus permitted service charges (HBP para 4.82(c)). — [DGFT FTP 2023 Chapter 4 PDF](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/a11b458e-0d61-460f-aef4-b201ed40a9aa/FTP2023_Chapter04.pdf); [DGFT HBP 2023 PDF](https://content.dgft.gov.in/Website/dgftprod/5484e173-16f0-4e2d-a8c0-089777734a59/HBP%20as%20on%2015.03.2023.pdf) (search snippets only)
- **Value addition and wastage numbers:** one snippet reports a minimum value addition of **3.5% for plain gold jewellery and 6-7% for studded gold jewellery**, and maximum wastage of **2.5% for plain vs 5% for studded**. — [taxguru: Value Addition Norms](https://taxguru.in/dgft/value-addition-norms-avail-duty-exemption-gem-jewellery.html); [taxguru: minimum value addition for studded jewellery](https://taxguru.in/dgft/minimum-addition-norms-calculation-addition-studded-jewellery-items.html); [taxguru: DGFT revised wastage limits](https://taxguru.in/dgft/dgft-notifies-revised-wastage-limits-input-norms-jewelry-exports.html). The 2015 tightening is covered by [Business Standard, 2 Apr 2015](https://www.business-standard.com/article/markets/govt-tightens-value-addition-norms-for-gold-jewellery-to-stop-circular-trading-115040200441_1.html). The search tool's paraphrase mixed in old (2004-09 HBP) figures such as 15% for studded, so **the current HBP 4.61 table must be checked directly**.
- Guides to duty-free gold/silver/platinum import under AA exist. — [Afleo](https://afleo.com/duty-free-import-of-gold-silver-platinum-advance-authorisation-scheme/); [TaxTMI AA deep dive](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=16112)

### Inferences
- With duty at 15%, domestic duty-paid gold is roughly 9 percentage points more expensive than a year earlier. A small D2C exporter will usually source gold one of three ways: (a) from a nominated agency (e.g., MMTC, banks, STC-type agencies) on loan or outright under the replenishment/export route, duty-free; (b) under their own AA, which is now capped at 100 kg per authorisation and has an inspection hurdle for first-timers; (c) by using a job worker or manufacturer located in SEEPZ-SEZ (Mumbai) or a Surat/other G&J SEZ, where gold enters duty-free. Route (c) is often the simplest for a brand without its own factory.
- **[unverified]** Gold jewellery generally has an all-industry drawback rate linked to the gold content, or is covered by duty-free inputs. Drawback on duty-paid gold is less efficient than duty-free sourcing.
- For a D2C brand, meeting the 3.5-7% value addition is trivial because retail margins are far higher. The binding constraints are paperwork and the AA cap and inspection.

## 3. E-commerce export policy: courier cap, DNK, e-commerce export hubs, jewellery returns, RoDTEP, IGST

### Takeaway
From **1 Apr 2026 CBIC removed the INR 10 lakh per-consignment cap on courier exports** (Circular 17/2026-Customs and the Courier Amendment Regulations 2026) and launched an ECCS "Return Module" with risk-based re-import of returns. **Precious and imitation jewellery exported via e-commerce was excluded from the risk-based re-import**, so jewellery returns still follow the stricter Circular 09/2022 procedure. RoDTEP ran until at least 30 Sep 2026, but gold jewellery gets little or nothing from it.

### Cited Findings
- **Courier cap removal:** CBIC Circular No. 17/2026-Customs abolished the INR 10 lakh per-consignment value cap for courier exports, effective **1 April 2026**, as a Union Budget 2026-27 announcement. It also created a separate ECCS "Return Module" for e-commerce returns and rejects, and replaced strict verification of re-imports with a risk-based framework. — [TaxTMI: Circular 17/2026](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=16128); [taxguru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/cbic-removed-rs-10-lakh-cap-courier-exports.html); [Mundhra Consulting: Courier Imports and Exports Amendment Regulations 2026](https://mundhraconsulting.com/current-updates/courier-imports-exports-amendment-regulations-2026/); [NewKerala](https://www.newkerala.com/news/a/cbic-operationalises-comprehensive-reforms-e-commerce-exports-courier-trade-188.htm); [A2Z Taxcorp](https://a2ztaxcorp.net/cbic-unveils-comprehensive-reforms-for-e-commerce-exports-and-courier-trade-under-union-budget-2026-27-removing-%E2%82%B910-lakh-cap-streamlining-logistics-and-boosting-ease-of-doing-busine/)
- **Jewellery excluded:** "precious and imitation jewellery exported via e-commerce have been excluded" from the risk-based re-import approach. GJEPC welcomed the cap removal but "urges extension of risk-based norms to jewellery". — [GJEPC Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/govt-removes-%E2%82%B910-lakh-cap-on-courier-export-gjepc-applauds-courier-reforms-but-urges-extension-of-risk-based-norms-to-jewellery/)
- **Jewellery return/re-import framework (2022):** Circular 09/2022 and Notification 57/2022-Cus (N.T.), both 30 Jun 2022, include a re-import process for returned jewellery exported on e-commerce platforms via courier. — [Taxscan](https://www.taxscan.in/cbic-amends-regulations-for-re-import-of-returned-jewellery-exported-through-courier-on-an-e-commerce-platform/188151/); [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2022/07/05/regulation-of-provisions-on-re-import-of-jewellery-exported-on-e-commerce-platforms-vide-courier-imports-and-exports-electronic-declaration-and-processing-amendment-regulations-2022/); [taxguru regulatory framework](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/regulatory-framework-e-commerce-exports-jewellery-courier-mode.html)
- **Dak Ghar Niryat Kendras:** they run hub-and-spoke with Foreign Post Offices, and 1,013 DNKs have been set up. **E-Commerce Export Hubs:** five pilot ECEHs are under development under a DGFT Trade Notice. The snippet says "Trade Notice No. 14/2025 dated 22 Aug 2024", an internally inconsistent citation to verify. The hubs co-locate customs, packaging, warehousing and certification. — [Devdiscourse](https://www.devdiscourse.com/article/business/3530635-e-commerce-export-hubs-to-boost-cross-border-trade-for-indian-smes-and-artisans); [IBEF](https://www.ibef.org/news/e-commerce-export-hubs-to-support-indian-smes-with-cost-effective-logistics-and-streamlined-regulatory-processes); [DD India, Sep 2026](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/09/districts-as-export-hubs-initiative-covers-over-770-districts-across-india/) (Districts as Export Hubs covers 770+ districts)
- **RoDTEP:** DGFT Notification 74/2025-26 (31 Mar 2026) extended RoDTEP from 1 Apr 2026 to **30 Sep 2026** at the existing Appendix 4R/4RE rates. Exports claiming duty drawback, and products on DGFT's exclusion list, are excluded. Gems and jewellery get low rates because raw material dominates cost. — [Eximpe](https://eximpe.com/blog/b2b/rodtep-scheme-in-2026-meaning-rates-claim-process-practical-guide-for-exporters); [Afleo RoDTEP rates](https://afleo.com/rodtep-rates-updated/); [Eximoz](https://blog.eximoz.com/blog/rodtep-rates-list-2026-hsn-wise-rate-table/)

### Inferences
- The cap removal matters for a D2C brand: high-value orders (e.g., a USD 15k tennis bracelet) or consolidated courier shipments to a US 3PL are no longer capped at INR 10 lakh.
- Returns are the pain point for D2C jewellery. The exclusion means returned gold/LGD pieces still go through the jewellery-specific re-import verification under Circular 09/2022 (proving identity with the export). Plan photo, weight and hallmark/laser-inscription records per SKU at export. Many Indian D2C exporters also hold returns in a US 3PL for resale rather than re-importing.
- RoDTEP expires on 30 Sep 2026 unless extended again. Check for a further extension after that date. Its value for gold jewellery is likely negligible either way. **[unverified]** Gold jewellery under AA or other duty-free input routes generally cannot claim RoDTEP.
- **[unverified]** RoSCTL covers apparel and made-ups only and does not apply to jewellery.
- IGST: exporting under a LUT (zero-rated) avoids IGST. On domestic purchases, GST is 3% on gold and jewellery and 0.25%/1.5% on diamonds **[unverified]**, and that ITC is refundable as unutilised ITC.

## 4. BIS hallmarking for exports

### Takeaway
BIS hallmarking is **not mandatory for exports**. Articles meant for export that conform to the foreign buyer's specification are exempt, as is jewellery exported and re-imported under the trade policy. For the US, the exporter must instead meet US standards (FTC Jewelry Guides and US karat stamping rules).

### Cited Findings
- "Articles meant for export that conform to any specification required by the foreign buyer are exempted from hallmarking", and jewellery "for export and re-import as per the trade policy" is also exempt. Guidelines are dated 24 Dec 2021. Other exemptions cover articles under 2 g and articles for international exhibitions. — [GJEPC news](https://www.gjepc.org/news_detail.php?news=gold-jewellery-exports-gold-articles-exempted-from-mandatory-hallmarking-1); [GJEPC Solitaire](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/gold-jewellery-exports-gold-articles-exempted-from-mandatory-hallmarking/); [Retail Jeweller India](https://retailjewellerindia.com/india-changes-jewellery-laws-creates-gold-hallmarking-exemptions/)
- GJEPC's framing: hallmarking is "an Indian consumer-specific regulation and exporters must comply with the hallmarking regulations of the country to which goods are exported". — same GJEPC sources
- Mandatory hallmarking for domestic sale was introduced in 2021. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=1727944); [BIS jewellers page](https://www.bis.gov.in/hallmarking-jewellers/?lang=en)

### Inferences
- If the brand also sells domestically in India, it would need BIS registration and HUID hallmarking for those pieces. **[unverified]** Indian standard grades include 14K (585), 18K (750) and 22K (916). US-bound pieces can be stamped 14K/18K to US tolerance.

### Gaps
- None critical.

## 5. Lab-grown diamond industry in India

### Takeaway
India, centred on Surat, is the world's main LGD cutting and polishing hub and a large CVD grower. LGD prices have collapsed: India's average LGD export price fell from about USD 389/ct in 2022 to about USD 71/ct in Jan-May 2026. That is a sourcing advantage for a jewellery brand. The US share of India's LGD exports fell sharply as trade shifted to Hong Kong.

### Cited Findings
- In FY2025-26, polished LGD exports fell 10.55% YoY to **USD 1.13 bn** even as volumes rose, which points to continuing price correction. — [GJEPC FY2025-26 press release](https://gjepc.org/news_detail.php?news=india-s-gem-jewellery-exports-in-fy-2025-26-at-usd-27-72-billion-signals-structural-reset-towards-broad-based-growth-reducing-dependence-on-a-single-market) (total G&J exports **USD 27.72 bn** in FY2025-26); [Heera Zhaveraat](https://heerazhaveraat.com/in-2025-26-indias-gems-and-jewelry-exports-indicate-a-shift-towards-broad-based-growth-reducing-reliance-on-any-single-market/)
- India's average LGD export price fell from **USD 388.77/ct (2022) to USD 71.13/ct (Jan-May 2026)**. The US share of India's LGD shipments fell from 63.49% (2022) to 16.66% (Jan-May 2026), while Hong Kong's rose from 13.51% to 45.4%. — [Business Standard Substack: India's diamond exports lose lustre](https://businessstandard.substack.com/p/indias-diamond-exports-lose-lustre)
- FY2025-26 LGD export volume was reportedly 18.8 M ct vs 16 M ct natural, the first full year in which LGD volume exceeded natural. — [Nishal Gems blog](https://www.nishalgems.com/post/india-lab-grown-diamond-exports-surpass-natural-diamonds-2026) (vendor blog, lower reliability)

## 8. Indian export response to US tariffs (2025-2026)

### Takeaway
The US imposed a **50% tariff on Indian goods from 27 Aug 2025**, and India's gem exports to the US fell about 44%. An India-US interim agreement framework (7 Feb 2026) outlined zero duty for diamonds and gemstones. Since **24 Jul 2026, a Section 301 regime keeps a 10% additional tariff** on Indian gems and jewellery, including LGDs and jewellery, on top of the 5.5-6% MFN duty on jewellery, for about **15.5-16% effective**. That is 2.5 points lower than China, Hong Kong, Thailand, the UAE and others. Exporters diversified to the UAE, Europe and Hong Kong.

### Cited Findings
- The 50% US tariffs took effect 27 Aug 2025 and cut India's gem exports to the US by 43.8%. GJEPC met FM Sitharaman seeking relief. — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/economy-and-policy/us-tariffs-weak-demand-hit-gems-and-jewellery-exports-gjepc-seeks-duty-relief-ahead-of-budget); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/business%2Feconomy%2Ftrump-tariff-effect-gem-jewellery-exporters-stare-at-severe-losses-3671206)

On 7 Feb 2026 the India-US Interim Agreement framework outlined zero-duty access for these categories. FIEO welcomed the finalisation of the India-US trade deal on 3 Feb 2026. — [GJEPC Solitaire: Zero-duty framework](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/a-new-dawn-for-india-us-trade-zero-duty-framework-to-revitalise-gem-jewellery-sector/); [News On Air, 3 Feb 2026](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/fieo-welcomes-finalisation-of-india-us-trade-deal/)
- The US replaced a temporary Section 122 surcharge with a **Section 301 regime with no automatic lapse**. It keeps a 10% additional duty on Indian exports, including natural diamonds, LGDs, coloured gems and jewellery, from **24 Jul 2026**, following a 23 Jul Presidential Memorandum after USTR's forced-labour Section 301 investigations of 60 economies. Jewellery bears 5.5-6% MFN + 10%, about 15.5-16% effective. India is in the lower 10% band because it adopted a forced-labour import prohibition, giving it a **2.5-point advantage** over China, Hong Kong, Thailand, Türkiye, the UAE, Israel and Vietnam. The US takes about USD 9.9 bn (about 35%) of India's G&J exports. — [GJEPC Solitaire: US retains 10% under Section 301](https://gjepc.org/solitaire/us-retains-10-tariff-on-indian-exports-under-new-section-301-regime/); [Retail Jeweller India](https://retailjewellerindia.com/us-imposes-10-section-301-tariff-on-indian-gem-and-jewellery-exports-raising-cost-pressures-in-key-market/); [InStore](https://instoremag.com/new-u-s-tariff-regime-puts-indias-gem-jewellery-exports-under-pressure-gjepc/); [DiamondWorld](https://www.diamondworld.net/news/gjepc-calls-for-tariff-relief-as-us-retains-10-duty-on-indian-gem-jewellery-imports)

### Inferences
- For a D2C exporter in Sep 2026, the US landed-duty burden on Indian gold/LGD jewellery is about 15.5-16% of customs value. The US de minimis exemption was suspended in 2025 **[unverified here; see the US-side research]**. Re-routing via the UAE no longer helps, because the UAE is in a higher Section 301 band than India according to GJEPC.
- The zero-duty Feb 2026 framework appears not to have been implemented for jewellery. Both GJEPC and trade press report the 10% still applies from 24 Jul 2026.
