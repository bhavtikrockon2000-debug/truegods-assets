# Indian Regulations, Duties and Compliance for Importing Kids' Toys from China (status as of 26 Sep 2026)

Research note: many primary sites (bis.gov.in, services.bis.gov.in, lexology, csagroup, worldtradescanner, eximpe) were blocked by the fetch proxy, so several findings below rest on search-result snippets of those pages, not full-page reads. They are flagged where this matters.

## 1. Toys (Quality Control) Order 2020: BIS/ISI certification, Chinese factories, testing

### Takeaway
All toys for children aged 14 and under must carry the ISI mark under a BIS licence held by the **actual manufacturing factory**. A foreign factory gets this licence through the Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme (FMCS), which takes about 6 months and needs a USD 10,000 bank guarantee. Up to at least 2023, **no Chinese toy factory had been granted a BIS toy licence**, even though about 160 had applied. BIS reportedly started processing Chinese FMCS applications again in 2025. I found no confirmed report of a Chinese toy factory actually receiving a licence. This is the single biggest barrier for a brand that wants to source toys from China.

### Cited Findings
- DPIIT issued the Toys (Quality Control) Order, 2020. It requires toys for children aged 14 years or younger to conform to 7 Indian Standards for toy safety and to carry the ISI mark under a BIS licence. It has been in force since 1 Jan 2021. — [BIS blog: Toys QCO](https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/BISBlog/toys-quality-control-order/) (search snippet)
- The QCO was issued on 25 Feb 2020 and brought toys under compulsory BIS certification from 1 Jan 2021. — [TaxGuru, "No Ban on Import of Toys"](https://taxguru.in/dgft/no-ban-import-toys.html)
- Under the QCO, "no person shall manufacture, import, distribute, sell, hire, lease, store or exhibit for sale any toys without the ISI mark." — [PIB release PRID 1896001 (Feb 2023)](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1896001&reg=3&lang=2)
- Applicable standards: the IS 9873 series covers mechanical/physical safety, flammability, migration of heavy elements and so on, and IS 15644 covers electric toys. Any toy that uses a power source (battery cars, drones, learning tablets, RC devices, robotic kits, musical toys) must also meet IS 15644:2006. — [Beacon Filing](https://beaconfiling.com/blog/bis-certification-electronic-toys); [Standphill](https://www.standphillindia.in/bis-certification-for-toys.php)
- **New standard:** IS 9873 (Part 1):2025, aligned with ISO 8124-1, runs alongside the 2019 version until **22 March 2027**. After that date BIS will grant licences only under the 2025 version. — [BIS blog (search snippet)](https://www.services.bis.gov.in/php/BIS_2.0/BISBlog/toys-quality-control-order/)
- **Chinese factories:** In Feb 2023 the government told Parliament that 29 licences had been granted under FMCS to foreign toy units and **none to any unit in China**. Vietnam held 14 of those licences. Three were granted in 2021-22 and 26 in 2022-23. — [PIB, 3 Feb 2023](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1896001&reg=3&lang=2); [Business Standard, Feb 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/article/economy-policy/bis-issued-29-licences-to-foreign-toy-manufacturing-units-says-govt-123020301364_1.html)
- About 160 Chinese toymakers had applied to BIS over two years, and none had been granted a licence. At that time BIS had licensed 982 domestic toy manufacturers. — [Business Standard, 6 Jan 2023](https://www.business-standard.com/article/companies/govt-yet-to-approve-bis-certificate-to-160-chinese-toy-companies-123010601021_1.html); [ThePrint, Jan 2023](https://theprint.in/economy/no-chinese-toymaker-has-bis-licence-official/1302774/)
- The reason given was that BIS officials could not visit China for factory inspections during and after the pandemic. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1896001&reg=3&lang=2)
- 2025 change: after nearly five years, BIS "will resume processing applications under FMCS for manufacturing facilities in China". FMCS approvals for Chinese plants had been frozen since early 2020. Inspections are reportedly being scheduled, with electronics, steel and consumer goods given priority. — [CSA Group regulatory update (search snippet; page not fetchable, exact date not verified)](https://www.csagroup.org/global-certification-regulatory-update/bis-resumes-processing-of-certifications-for-chinese-manufacturers/); [SS Global (consultant)](https://www.ssglobalservices.in/blogs/bis-certification-for-chinese-products)
- FMCS terms: licence is granted in about 6 months from a complete application (can take longer because of inspection or testing delays). A **USD 10,000 Performance Bank Guarantee** from a bank with an RBI-approved Indian branch is required after grant. The licence is valid for 1-2 years at first and renewable for up to 5 years. — [BIS FMCS Grant of Licence page (snippet)](https://www.bis.gov.in/fmcs/certification-process/grant-of-licence/?lang=en); [Aleph India](https://alephindia.in/foreign-manufacturers-certification-scheme-fmcs.php)
- **Consignment testing at ports:** DGFT Notification No. 33/2015-20 dated 2 Dec 2019 required a random sample from every imported toy consignment to be tested at an NABL-accredited lab, with no sale allowed until the test passed. Consignments that fail are sent back or destroyed at the importer's cost. — [DGFT Notification 33 PDF (NABL)](https://nabl-india.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/12/DGFT-Toys-Notification-No-33-dt-02.12.2019-English-1.pdf); [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/dgft/no-ban-import-toys.html)
- **2026 change (partly supersedes the above):** DGFT Notification No. 64/2025-26 dated **18 Mar 2026** deleted Policy Condition No. 2(iii) of Chapter 95, ITC(HS) 2022 Schedule-1, with immediate effect. Commentators describe this as removing a layer of mandatory import-point documentation or testing for toys. BIS/QCO compliance still applies. — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/notifications?id=145249); [Centax](https://www.centaxonline.com/blog/dgft-deletes-import-policy-condition-under-chapter-95); [InstaCertify](https://instacertify.com/resources/blogs/dgft-removes-key-import-condition-for-toys)
- A QCI survey found that about 67% of imported toys were unsafe for children. This was part of the rationale for the regime. — [The Hawk](https://www.thehawk.in/news/health/nearly-67percent-of-imported-toys-are-dangerous-for-kids:-qci-survey)
- The broader QCO rollback of Nov 2025 removed mandatory BIS certification for some **inputs** (textiles, plastics, metals). The toys QCO was not among those withdrawn. QCOs had grown from 14 items in 2017 to about 800 by 2025. — [Tribune/GTRI](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/withdrawal-of-qcos-eases-costs-for-msmes-but-opens-floodgates-to-cheap-chinese-imports-gtri)
- India's toy exports rose 89% (DD India, Jul 2026). The government credits the QCO and duty push for the growth of domestic manufacturing. — [DD India, Jul 2026](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/07/indias-toy-exports-surge-89-as-government-push-boosts-domestic-manufacturing-quality-standards/)

### Inferences
- The BIS licence belongs to the factory, not the brand. A D2C brand therefore has to pick Chinese suppliers that already hold (or will get) an FMCS toy licence for each product and standard. Otherwise it has to shift sourcing to Vietnam or India, or have the product made by a BIS-licensed Indian manufacturer. As of 2023 this made "China-made, ISI-marked toys" practically unavailable. The 2025 resumption may change that, but only slowly, one factory at a time.
- Budget for 6+ months per factory plus a USD 10k bank guarantee, testing, inspection travel costs and consultant fees. The IS 9873-1:2025 transition means licences should be taken or migrated to the 2025 version before 22 Mar 2027.
- The exact scope of the deleted Condition 2(iii) should be confirmed with a customs broker. If it was the per-consignment testing rule, clearance for licensed product may now be faster.

### Gaps
- There is no verified count of Chinese toy factories holding BIS licences in 2025-26. I found no news report of a first Chinese toy licence.
- I could not retrieve the exact text of DGFT Notification 64/2025-26 or of the original Condition 2(iii).
- I could not verify BIS application, marking or annual fees in INR for toys, or lab testing costs per standard.
- I could not fetch the full list of 7 standards and the exemptions (for example, handmade toys by artisans or GI-registered sellers are exempt under the QCO amendment). This should be checked on bis.gov.in.

## 2. Customs duty and GST on toys (HS 9503)

### Takeaway
Finished toys under HS 9503 carry **70% Basic Customs Duty**. Social Welfare Surcharge (SWS) on toys was **abolished from 2 Feb 2026** (Budget 2026-27). GST/IGST dropped from 12% to **5% for non-electronic toys from 22 Sep 2025** (GST 2.0). Electronic toys remain at **18%**. The effective import duty is now about 78.5% on CIF for regular toys and about 100.6% for electronic toys (my calculations below). IGST is creditable.

### Cited Findings
- BCD on toys went from 20% to 60% in Feb 2020 and then to **70% in 2023** (Budget 2023-24). — [KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/others/customs-duty-revisions-to-boost-indias-electronic-toy-manufacturing-sector-budget-2025); [EximGuru tariff 95030090](https://www.eximguru.com/indian-customs-duty/95030090-tricycles-scooters-pedal-cars-and.aspx)
- Budget 2025-26: parts of electronic toys (9503 00 91) for manufacturing were set at 20% BCD plus 7.5% AIDC. Finished toys stayed at 70%. — [KNN India, Feb 2025](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/others/customs-duty-revisions-to-boost-indias-electronic-toy-manufacturing-sector-budget-2025)
- Budget 2026-27: "All goods under heading 9503, including parts of electronic toys for manufacture of electronic toys" are exempt from SWS, effective **2 Feb 2026**. — [TaxGuru Budget 2026 customs](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/proposed-key-customs-central-excise-budget-2026.html); [BDO Customs rate chart 2026](https://www.bdo.in/en-gb/insights/alerts-updates/customs-duty-rate-chart-2026)
- GST 2.0 (56th GST Council, 3 Sep 2025; effective **22 Sep 2025**): the 12% and 28% slabs were abolished, leaving 5% and 18% as the main slabs. GST on toys (including tricycles, scooters, pedal cars and their parts) was cut from 12% to **5%**. — [PIB factsheet](https://www.pib.gov.in/FactsheetDetails.aspx?Id=150302&reg=48&lang=2); [GST Council press release](https://gstcouncil.gov.in/sites/default/files/2025-09/press_release_press_information_bureau.pdf); [Kotak MF](https://www.kotakmf.com/Information/blogs/gst-2-point-0)
- Electronic or battery-operated toys attract **18%** GST (Tamil Nadu AAR earlier held 18% for electronic toys versus 12% for other toys). The 2026 rate listings show 5% for basic toys and 18% for electronic toys. — [TaxGuru on TN AAR](https://taxguru.in/goods-and-service-tax/18-percent-gst-applicable-electronic-toys-12-percent-toys.html); [Busy.in](https://busy.in/gst-rates/toys/)
- IGST at import = (assessable value + BCD + SWS) × IGST rate. — [iWishBag primer](https://www.iwishbag.com/in/guides/india-customs-duty-primer)
- Note: one aggregator claimed "AIDC 70%" on toys. This looks like an error; no AIDC on finished toys appears in any other source. Treat it as unreliable. — [search result aggregating EximPe/others](https://eximpe.com/hsn/toys-hsn-code)

### Inferences (my calculations, not sourced)
- Non-electronic toy, from 2 Feb 2026: BCD 70% + SWS 0 + IGST 5% on (100+70) = 8.5, giving about **78.5% of CIF**. IGST (8.5) is creditable against output GST, so the sunk duty cost is about **70%**.
- Electronic toy (18% IGST): 70 + 0.18×170 = 30.6, giving about **100.6% of CIF**. The sunk cost is still 70% if IGST is credited.
- History for comparison: before 22 Sep 2025 the calculation was BCD 70 + SWS 7 (10% of BCD) + IGST 12% × 177 = 21.24, about 98.2% of CIF. Between 22 Sep 2025 and 1 Feb 2026 it was 70 + 7 + 5% × 177 = 8.85, about 85.9% of CIF.
- Assessable value includes freight and insurance (CIF), so landed cost ≈ FOB × ~1.1 × 1.785 before port, broker, testing and inland charges.

### Gaps
- I did not verify the CBIC tariff directly for 2026-27 (the site was not fetched). The 70% BCD should be confirmed on the ICEGATE/CBIC tariff at the time of import.
- The exact HS split between "electronic toys" at 18% and other toys at 5% should be confirmed per SKU.

## 3. Restrictions on imports from China and India-China trade climate

### Takeaway
There is no ban on importing Chinese toys. The binding constraints are non-tariff barriers: the BIS/QCO factory licence requirement, historical per-consignment testing, and enforcement against misdeclaration. Press Note 3 applies to **investment (FDI)** from China, not to trading imports, and it was partly relaxed in 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Press Note 3 (2020) required government approval for FDI from land-bordering countries. It was partly eased by **Press Note 2 (2026)**, which has been operational since **1 May 2026**. Investors with up to 10% Chinese beneficial ownership can now use the automatic route, and some manufacturing proposals are to be decided within 60 days. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/how-india-press-note-3-revision-unlocks-china-linked-capital-2026-43449.html/); [Legal500](https://www.legal500.com/developments/thought-leadership/india-opens-the-door-to-chinese-investment-a-complete-guide-to-press-note-2-2026-and-the-fema-ndi-amendments/); [Carnegie, Apr 2026](https://carnegieendowment.org/research/2026/04/indias-press-note-3-gamble-opening-the-fdi-door-to-china)
- India's trade deficit with China was USD 99 bn in FY2024-25 and over USD 112 bn in FY2025-26. — [India Briefing](https://www.india-briefing.com/news/how-india-press-note-3-revision-unlocks-china-linked-capital-2026-43449.html/)
- There is "no ban on import of toys"; the government's approach is quality control. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/dgft/no-ban-import-toys.html)
- India's China policy in 2026 is described as "selective derisking". — [East Asia Forum, 28 Jul 2026](https://eastasiaforum.org/2026/07/28/indias-china-doctrine-of-selective-derisking/)

### Inferences
- Press Note 3 matters only if a Chinese partner takes equity in the Indian brand. It does not affect a 100% Indian-owned importer.
- I found no anti-dumping duty specifically on finished toys (HS 9503) in the sources reviewed.

### Gaps
- I could not confirm any current anti-dumping duties on toys or on toy inputs from China.
- I found no quantified data on 2025-26 port clearance delays for Chinese toys.

## 4. Other rules: IEC, Legal Metrology, EPR, electronics/WPC, infant products

### Takeaway
An importer-brand needs an IEC, LMPC importer registration and MRP labelling, and CPCB EPR (plastic packaging) registration. Customs has been blocking non-EPR consignments since Jul 2025. Toy-adjacent baby products such as feeding bottles fall under their own BIS QCOs.

### Cited Findings
- Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules 2011: importers of pre-packaged goods must hold **LMPC registration**. The principal display panel must show the name and address of the importer or responsible person, net quantity, **MRP inclusive of taxes** and other declarations. — [TaxTMI article](https://www.taxtmi.com/article/detailed?id=17256); [PCN India, 12 Jun 2026](https://pcnindiaglobal.com/2026/06/12/importing-products-to-india-approvals-certifications-licences/)
- EPR under the Plastic Waste Management Rules applies to producers, importers and brand owners (PIBOs). From **1 Jul 2025** (rule 11(1A)), plastic packaging must show the PIBO's name and CPCB EPR registration number (by barcode, QR code or similar). — [Aleph India](https://alephindia.in/mandatory-labelling-on-plastic-packaging-as-per-cpcb-notification.php); [Corpseed](https://www.corpseed.com/knowledge-centre/labelling-requirements-under-cpcb-epr-plastic-and-plastic-waste-management-pwm-guidelines)
- Since Jul 2025, consignments from importers of plastic-packaged goods who lack valid EPR registration are reportedly being blocked at customs. — [Afleo (consultant, 2026)](https://afleo.com/epr-registration-for-plastic-waste-management/) (single consultant source; not verified against a CBIC circular)
- Electric toys need IS 15644 in addition to IS 9873. — [Beacon Filing](https://beaconfiling.com/blog/bis-certification-electronic-toys)
- Plastic feeding bottles need a mandatory ISI mark under **IS 14625:2015**, which includes BPA restrictions. Teats and soothers have their own standards. Stainless steel feeding bottles are covered by IS 18800. — [EVTL India](https://evtlindia.com/blog/bis-certification-for-feeding-bottles-india); [Standphill](https://www.standphillindia.in/product/bis-certification-stainless-steel-feeding-bottles-is-18800.php)

### Inferences
- A brand selling teethers, silicone feeding sets or plates next to its toys should check each SKU for classification: a teether may be a toy (IS 9873), while a feeding item may fall under IS 14625 or food-contact rules. Each classification brings its own certification.
- RC toys using radio frequencies probably need WPC ETA (self-declaration) approval. I found no source in this session to confirm this.
- Battery Waste Management Rules EPR probably applies to toys that include batteries (not sourced).

### Gaps
- I found no source on the WPC/ETA requirement for RC toys, Battery EPR for toys, or FSSAI food-contact rules for teethers.
- I did not source IEC (DGFT, online, free) procedure details in this session.

## 5. Marketing claims: "non-toxic", "BPA-free", "eco-friendly"

### Takeaway
The CCPA Greenwashing Guidelines (notified **15 Oct 2024**) name "non-toxic", "free-of", "100% natural", "plastic-free" and similar claims as ones that must be backed by credible certification or scientific or third-party evidence. Vague terms such as "eco-friendly", "green" and "sustainable" used without qualifiers are prohibited. The guidelines also cover advertising agencies and endorsers.

### Cited Findings
- CCPA "Guidelines for Prevention and Regulation of Greenwashing or Misleading Environmental Claims, 2024" were notified on 15 Oct 2024 under the Consumer Protection Act 2019. — [PIB PRID 2064963](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2064963&reg=48&lang=2)
- Claims such as "compostable", "degradable", "free-of", "**non-toxic**", "100% natural", "recyclable", "plastic-free" and "net-zero" require disclosure of credible certification, reliable scientific evidence, verifiable internal evidence, or statutory or independent third-party verification. — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2064963&reg=48&lang=2); [Chandhiok & Mahajan alert](https://www.chandhiok.com/post/c-m-e-alert-the-guidelines-for-prevention-and-regulation-of-greenwashing-or-misleading-environmenta)
- Generic terms ("green", "eco-friendly", "good for the planet", "carbon neutral") used without adequate qualifiers are prohibited. The guidelines apply to manufacturers, advertisers, ad agencies and endorsers. — [Lexology summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3dbbdb30-1392-4a28-a20f-75b08a263742); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/ccpa-issues-guidelines-against-greenwashing-misleading-environmental-claims-45040.htm)
- Industry has called the guidelines welcome but says enforcement is needed. — [Mongabay, Oct 2024](https://india.mongabay.com/2024/10/greenwashing-guidelines-a-welcome-step-but-needs-enforcement-says-industry/)

### Inferences
- "Non-toxic" and "BPA-free" (a "free-of" claim) should be backed by named lab reports (for example IS 9873-3 heavy-element migration, or EN 71-3/ASTM F963 as supporting evidence) and disclosed on the pack or website, for example through a QR code linking to the reports.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the text of the ASCI guidelines on environmental claims (2023) or penalty amounts under the Consumer Protection Act. From general knowledge, CCPA can impose up to Rs 10 lakh for a first offence and up to Rs 50 lakh for repeat offences for misleading ads; this was not verified this session.

## 6. Penalties and enforcement examples

### Takeaway
BIS and DRI actively enforce the toy QCO. Raids have hit e-commerce warehouses and misdeclared Chinese consignments, and non-compliant toys are seized and destroyed or re-exported at the importer's cost.

### Cited Findings
- On 7 Mar 2025 BIS raided Amazon and Flipkart warehouses in Delhi, Gurugram and Lucknow. At Amazon Lucknow it found **215 toys** and 24 hand blenders without BIS certification. — [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/india/bis-cracks-down-on-amazon-flipkart-for-selling-unsafe-items); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/govts-e-commerce-crackdown-bis-raids-amazon-flipkart-warehouses-seizes-products-worth-rs-70-lakh)
- On 19 Mar 2025 BIS Chennai raided Amazon and Flipkart warehouses and seized uncertified toys, bottles, fans, diapers and other goods. About 3,376 products worth Rs 36 lakh were seized at Amazon Durainallur, and the Delhi branch seized goods worth Rs 76 lakh. — [PIB PRID 2113173](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2113173); [MediaNama, Mar 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/03/223-amazon-flipkart-warehouses-raided-again-bis-seizes-76-lakh-non-certified-products/)
- On 24 Jul 2025 DRI Mumbai seized **160 MT** of Chinese toys, cosmetics and shoes worth Rs 6.5 crore from 10 containers at Mundra, Hazira, Kandla SEZ and ICD Piyala. The goods were misdeclared as mini plants, keychains and pencil boxes, and the toys had no BIS certification, violating the FTP and the Toys QCO. — [TaxTMI](https://www.taxtmi.com/news?id=50909); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fchinese-toys-cosmetics-shoes-worth-over-rs-6-crore-seized-by-directorate-of-revenue-intelligence-3656887)
- Non-compliant toys are destroyed or re-exported at the importer's cost. — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/dgft/no-ban-import-toys.html)

### Inferences
- Marketplaces now remove listings without an ISI/CM/L licence number. A D2C brand should display the factory's BIS licence number on the pack and on listings.

### Gaps
- Statutory BIS Act 2016 penalty amounts for QCO violations were not verified in this session. From general knowledge, they are imprisonment up to 2 years and/or a fine of at least Rs 2 lakh, rising to 10 times the value of goods for repeat offences; this should be confirmed.
