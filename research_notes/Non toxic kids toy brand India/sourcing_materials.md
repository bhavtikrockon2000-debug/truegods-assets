# Sourcing Non-Toxic Kids' Toys (0-15) from China vs India for an Indian D2C Brand: Materials, Certifications, Costs (2025-2026)

Research note, written 2026-09-26. Several useful pages (Outlook Business, DD India, Melikey, dtsupplier, oyeexpress) could not be opened because the network blocked them. For those pages, the facts below come from search-result snippets, and the snippets are cited to the original URL. Treat snippet-only facts as lower confidence.

## 0. Headline finding for the report writer (read first)

### Takeaway
For an Indian D2C brand, the main limit on sourcing from China is regulation, not price. Under the Toys (Quality Control) Order, every toy sold in India needs a BIS licence tied to the specific factory that made it. The latest official data found (Feb 2023) says 29 foreign toy factories held BIS licences and none of them was in China, even though about 160 Chinese toymakers had applied. For practical purposes, a "Made in China" private-label toy cannot be sold legally in India today unless that specific Chinese factory holds a BIS licence. I found no evidence that any Chinese toy factory has one.

### Cited Findings
- Under the BIS Foreign Manufacturers Certification Scheme, 29 licences were granted to foreign toy factories and none to a unit in China (PIB press release, Feb 2023) — [PIB](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1896001&reg=3&lang=2)
- The BIS Director General said no Chinese toymaker holds a BIS licence. About 160 Chinese toymakers had applied in the two years before that statement and none was approved — [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/no-chinese-toymaker-has-bis-licence-official/1302774/); [VARIndia](https://varindia.com/news/govt-yet-to-approve-bis-certificates-to-chinese-toy-companies)
- The Toys QCO was introduced in 2020 and took effect in January 2021. It made BIS certification mandatory for toys sold in India. A 2019 government survey found only 33% of toys on the market met BIS standards. A 2025 BIS survey found 95% compliance — [Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/upstox-originals/investing/not-just-child-s-play-india-s-239-export-surge-explained/article-181466/) (search snippet)
- A foreign manufacturer applies under BIS FMCS and must appoint an Authorized Indian Representative (AIR). BIS publishes its list of licensees, searchable by IS 9873, on its website and mobile app — [Diligence Certification](https://www.diligencecertification.com/bis-certification-for-toys/) (search snippet)
- Customs guidance on BIS compliance when importing toys and toy parts — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/custom-duty/compliance-bis-standards-toys-parts-toys-import-reg.html)
- Basic customs duty (BCD) on toys went from 20% to 60% in Feb 2020 and to 70% in 2023. Budget 2025-26 cut BCD on **parts** of electronic toys (tariff item 9503 00 91) to 20% plus 7.5% AIDC, down from 25% — [KNN India](https://knnindia.co.in/news/newsdetails/sectors/others/customs-duty-revisions-to-boost-indias-electronic-toy-manufacturing-sector-budget-2025); [SEAIR](https://www.seair.co.in/blog/toys-import-from-china-to-india.aspx) (snippets)
- Toy imports from China fell from about $235M in FY20 to $40.2M in FY25, a drop of more than 83%. Total Indian toy imports fell from $371.7M (FY19) to $232.3M (FY26). Exports rose from $203.5M to $384.7M (+89%) — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/indias-toy-takeover-can-it-finally-beat-china-and-capture-5-of-the-global-market); [DD India, Jul 2026](https://ddindia.co.in/2026/07/indias-toy-exports-surge-89-as-government-push-boosts-domestic-manufacturing-quality-standards/) (snippets)
- IS 9873 (Part 1):2025 (the revised standard for non-electric toys) runs alongside IS 9873 (Part 1):2019 until 22 March 2027 — [Diligence Certification](https://www.diligencecertification.com/bis-certification-for-toys/) (snippet)

### Inferences
- In practice, "sourcing from China" for the Indian market can only mean: (a) using a Chinese factory that has obtained a BIS licence (none found); (b) importing Chinese **raw materials or components** (silicone compound, packaging, electronic toy parts at 20% BCD) and assembling or finishing in a BIS-licensed Indian unit; or (c) making the product in a third country whose factory holds BIS FMCS. Options (b) and (c) need legal confirmation. The legal licence holder is the factory where the toy is manufactured, so relabelling does not help.
- The BIS licence is tied to the factory, not the brand. A D2C brand therefore has to pick contract manufacturers that already hold a licence for IS 9873 (and IS 15644 for electric toys), or pay for that factory to get one.
- Baby-product edge cases such as silicone teethers and feeding items may fall outside the toy classification (HS 9503). This is a known grey area and was not verified here. A customs or BIS consultant should classify each SKU.

### Gaps
- I found no official 2025-26 statement updating the "zero Chinese toy licences" figure. One search snippet said BIS resumed licences for Chinese makers in other product categories in 2025, but it was unsourced and unverified.
- I did not verify the GST rate on toys after the Sept 2025 GST rationalisation. It was 12% before; some reports suggest 5%. Please confirm.

## 1. Safe / non-toxic materials by age group and what "non-toxic" means technically

### Takeaway
In technical terms, "non-toxic" means passing chemical limits: migration limits for 19 elements under EN 71-3 (lead, cadmium, mercury, chromium VI and others), restrictions on phthalates and PAH under REACH, and BPA migration limits. Plant-fibre "bioplastics" such as bamboo or wheat-straw mixed with melamine have a documented problem with formaldehyde and melamine leaching.

### Cited Findings
- EN 71-3 limits migration of 19 elements: Al, Sb, As, Ba, B, Cd, Cr(III), Cr(VI), Co, Cu, Pb, Mn, Hg, Ni, Se, Sr, Sn, organic tin, Zn — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/toys-childrens-products/eu-toy-safety-directive-chemical-safety-requirements/); [law.resource.org EN71-3 text](https://law.resource.org/pub/eu/toys/en.71.3.2015.html)
- EN 71-3:2019+A2:2024 took effect in June 2025 and replaced +A1:2021 — [UL Solutions](https://www.ul.com/news/toys-and-childrens-products-eu-cen-approves-updated-toy-safety-standard-en-71-32019a22024); [Intertek 2025 bulletin](https://www.intertek.com/products-retail/insight-bulletins/2025/1455/)
- EU toys must comply with REACH Annex XVII Entries 51/52 (phthalates) and Entry 50 (PAH) — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/toys-childrens-products/eu-toy-safety-directive-chemical-safety-requirements/) (snippet)
- The BPA migration limit is 0.04 mg/l for toys for under-3s and mouthed toys. From 1 Aug 2030, the new EU Toy Safety Regulation (EU) 2025/2509 lowers it to 0.005 mg/l and applies it to all toys — [Measurlabs](https://measurlabs.com/blog/bisphenol-a-testing-requirements-in-the-eu/); [Eurofins](https://www.eurofins.com/toys-hardlines/resources/articles/understanding-the-new-eu-toy-safety-regulation-eu-20252509-and-en-71-updates/); [SGS compromise text, Jul 2025](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2025/07/safeguards-09825-eu-announces-compromise-text-for-draft-toy-safety-regulation)
- Bioplastic controversy: bamboo, rice husk, wheat straw and hemp fillers are not authorised in EU food-contact plastics. They can cause formaldehyde and melamine to migrate above legal limits. An EU coordinated action logged 748 cases (644 on the market, 104 rejected at the border). Bamboo-fibre cups tested at 3.5x the melamine limit and 25x the formaldehyde limit — [European Commission "Bamboo-zling"](https://food.ec.europa.eu/food-safety/eu-agri-food-fraud-network/eu-coordinated-actions/bamboo-zling_en); [UK FSA via Food Safety Magazine](https://www.food-safety.com/articles/9639-uk-fsa-plastic-utensils-containing-bamboo-leach-formaldehyde-melamine-into-food); [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/knowledge-hub/technical-updates/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/uk-plastic-utensils-containing-bamboo-or-similar-materials-are-no-longer-allowed-in-commerce); [SGS 2021](https://www.sgs.com/en/news/2021/03/safeguards-03021-several-eu-member-states-announce-the-prohibition-of-food-contact-bamboo)
- The UK bans plastic utensils containing bamboo or similar plant materials — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/en/knowledge-hub/technical-updates/consumer-products-and-retail-essentials/uk-plastic-utensils-containing-bamboo-or-similar-materials-are-no-longer-allowed-in-commerce)
- Chinese teether suppliers market "FSC-certified wood and LFGB-approved silicone" as their safety claims (for example Lovekids) — [Melikey](https://www.melikeysilicone.com/news/silicone-teether-manufacturers-in-china/) (snippet)
- Channapatna toys use ivory wood (Aale mara) finished with lac coloured with vegetable dyes such as turmeric and indigo. Etikoppaka toys use turned wood with lacquer coloured by seed, root and leaf dyes — [DirectCreate](https://www.directcreate.com/craft/chenapatna-toys); [Etikoppaka (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etikoppaka)

### Inferences
- A suggested material map for a brand positioned as non-toxic:
  - **0-3 years (mouthing):** platinum-cured food-grade silicone (LFGB-grade), natural rubber, and uncoated or water-based-finished hardwood.
  - **3-8 years:** FSC wood with EN 71-3-compliant water-based paints; PP/ABS with phthalate-free test reports; organic-cotton plush (GOTS) with azo-free dyes.
  - **8-15 years:** STEM kits in ABS/PP. Watch battery and electrical rules (IS 15644 in India) and magnet rules.
- Avoid "wheat-straw / bamboo-fibre plastic" claims for anything a child can mouth. They carry reputational and regulatory risk.
- "Natural lac dye" crafts are a marketing positive, but still need migration testing. Natural pigments can contain heavy metals too.

### Gaps
- I found no sourced data comparing platinum-cured and peroxide-cured silicone (VOC residue), or on natural-rubber latex allergen limits. Specific limits for formaldehyde in wood (EN 71-9/-10/-11) and for azo dyes in textiles were not retrieved.

## 2. Standards (EN71, ASTM F963, CPSIA, ISO 8124, IS 9873), labs and testing costs

### Takeaway
Testing one SKU at a third-party lab costs roughly $420-680 for ASTM F963 alone, and $800-3,000 for a combined US and EU scope (2025-26 quotes). Indian BIS testing is a separate step because it is tied to the factory licence.

### Cited Findings
- JJR Lab (ISO/IEC 17025 accredited, in China) charges $420-570 for ASTM F963-23 — [JJR Lab](https://www.jjrlab.com/news/how-much-does-the-astm-f963-23-toy-testing-cost.html)
- ASTM F963 for a single-design plush toy (no batteries or scents) costs $420-680 per lot. A combined US and EU (ASTM + EN71) plush programme costs $800-2,400. Running both on the same lot saves 20-30% — [plushtoys-factory.com, 2026](https://plushtoys-factory.com/plush-toy-safety-testing-cost/)
- A general range of $800-3,000 per product. SGS, Intertek and BV quote per product — [CPS Toys EN71 guide](https://www.cps-toys.com/blog/en71-certification-for-toys-what-it-covers-how-to-get-it.html)
- Cost calculator covering EN71, ASTM F963 and ISO 8124 — [ECQA](https://ecqa.com/toy-testing-price-calculator/)
- Intertek offers toy testing services — [Intertek](https://www.intertek.com/toys-childrens-products/premiums/)
- Indian standards: IS 9873 covers non-electric toys and IS 15644 covers electric toys — [Standphill](https://www.standphillindia.in/bis-certification-for-toys.php)

### Inferences
- EN71 or ASTM reports from a Chinese factory help with product development, but they do **not** replace a BIS licence for sale in India.

### Gaps
- I did not find BIS licence cost and timeline figures (fees, factory audit, AIR costs) from authoritative sources.

## 3. Chinese clusters, sourcing channels, FOB prices, MOQs, tooling

### Takeaway
Yunhe dominates wooden toys, Chenghai dominates plastic, and Yangzhou dominates plush. Silicone teethers sell for about $0.55-3.35 FOB with MOQs of 20-1,000 pieces. Detailed per-item FOB prices for the other categories could not be verified.

### Cited Findings
- **Yunhe (Lishui, Zhejiang):** about 40% of the global wooden-toy market and 56% of China's wooden toys. It has 1,100+ wooden-toy firms and 30,000+ workers. Output was ¥6.69bn (about $915M) in 2023, and more than 70% is exported to 82 countries — [China Daily, Feb 2025](https://epaper.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202502/13/WS67ad3c9ba3101a6334812cbb.html); [China Daily Dec 2025 (smart transformation)](https://subsites.chinadaily.com.cn/ezhejiang/2025-12/09/c_1146745.htm)
- **Chenghai (Shantou, Guangdong):** toy exports were ¥10.24bn in 2024 (72% of the district's exports). It is cited as making more than 50% of China's plastic toys and about one-third of global supply, with 300,000+ new models a year — [GDToday](https://goguangdong.southcn.com/node_7684524c17/594a554cbc.shtml); [Jingsourcing 2026 guide](https://jingsourcing.com/b-china-toys-wholesale-market/) (snippets)
- **Yangzhou (Jiangsu):** China's plush-toy hub. No share figures were found — [yiwuagt](https://www.yiwuagt.com/news/best-11-china-plush-toy-manufacturers-and-production-base/); [China Toy Expo 2025](https://cn.china-toy-expo.com/en/media_center/17384)
- **Silicone teethers:** $0.55-3.35 per piece with MOQs of 20-1,000 per colour. Another listing shows $5.56-6.20 with a 200-piece MOQ. JUTION Silicone offers a 100-piece MOQ — [made-in-china teether list](https://m.made-in-china.com/catlist/Teether-Toy-2203060000-46.html); [JUTION](https://jutionsilicone.com/oem/silicone-teether-toy); [customsiliconeoem MOQ 100](https://www.customsiliconeoem.com/products/silicone-teether-toy) (snippets, 2025)
- Channels include Global Sources, Made-in-China, Alibaba, and the Chenghai and Yiwu wholesale markets — [Global Sources](https://www.globalsources.com/manufacturers/silicone-toys.html); [GDToday](https://goguangdong.southcn.com/node_7684524c17/594a554cbc.shtml)

### Inferences
- Dongguan and Shenzhen are widely known for silicone and electronics, and Ningbo for toy exports, but this session did not retrieve data to confirm that.

## 4. Quality and safety risks, inspection, recalls

### Takeaway
Pre-shipment inspections cost about $200-310 per man-day. US recalls of Chinese-made toys sold on Amazon, involving lead, magnets and small parts, continued in 2025-26.

### Cited Findings
- QIMA pre-shipment inspection starts at $309 per man-day in China (Zone A), with travel and reporting bundled. ## 5. Landed-cost math (China → India)

### Takeaway
Import duty and taxes add roughly 80-100% to the CIF cost before any BIS work. That wipes out most of China's price advantage over Indian production, even in the hypothetical case where the licensing barrier is solved.

### Cited Findings
- BCD on toys is 70% — [SEAIR](https://www.seair.co.in/blog/toys-import-from-china-to-india.aspx); [Patron Accounting](https://www.patronaccounting.com/blog/how-to-import-toys-in-india) (snippets)

### Inferences (illustrative, my own arithmetic)
- Assumptions: $1.00 FOB silicone teether; freight and insurance at 10%, giving CIF $1.10; BCD 70% = $0.77; Social Welfare Surcharge at 10% of BCD = $0.077.
- Duties so far total $0.847, so the subtotal is $1.947.
- IGST on the subtotal is 5% ($0.097) or 12% ($0.234), depending on the rate after GST 2.0. That gives about **$2.04-2.18 landed, roughly 1.9-2.0x FOB**, before BIS, testing, inspection, clearing and domestic freight.
- At about ₹88/$, a $1 FOB teether costs about ₹180-190 landed. The FX rate is an assumption; check the current rate.

### Gaps
- The SWS and IGST rates and the ₹ rate are assumptions and were not verified in this session.

## 6. Indian alternatives

### Takeaway
India now has policy tailwinds (QCO, 70% BCD, a toy-cluster scheme announced in Budget 2025-26), large-scale clusters (Koppal/Aequs), and GI-tagged natural wooden crafts (Channapatna, Etikoppaka, Kondapalli). "Made in India" plus GI-tagged craft is a real positioning asset. Indian labour is cheaper, but factories are less efficient than China's.

### Cited Findings
- Budget 2025-26 announced a scheme, building on the National Action Plan for Toys, to make India a global toy hub. It covers clusters, skills and a manufacturing ecosystem for "high-quality, unique, innovative and sustainable toys that will represent the 'Made in India' brand" — [Business Standard, 1 Feb 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/budget/news/budget-2025-govt-to-implement-scheme-to-make-india-toy-hub-of-world-125020101069_1.html); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/budget-2025-26-fm-sitharaman-announces-measures-to-make-india-a-global-toy-hub/); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/budget-2025-govt-to-launch-scheme-for-toy-manufacturing/)
- Koppal (Karnataka): Aequs is investing about $500M in a 400-acre toy cluster in an SEZ, planned for about 100 units, with integrated moulding, tooling, painting, electronics and packaging. Aequs makes Disney-licensed toys priced at about ₹300-400 per piece — [Invest India](https://www.investindia.gov.in/team-india-blogs/karnatakas-koppal-toy-manufacturing-cluster-game-changer); [Manufacturing Today India](https://www.manufacturingtodayindia.com/aequs-ventures-into-disney-toys-at-koppal-toy-cluster); [Kyrascope, Jun 2026](https://www.kyrascope.com/toy-news/2026-06-07-aequs-koppal-toy-cluster-400-acre-make-in-india-export-hub-karnataka)
- Channapatna toys got a GI tag in 2005 and support 1,500+ artisans. Materials are ivory wood, lac and vegetable dyes — [DirectCreate](https://www.directcreate.com/craft/chenapatna-toys); [Karnataka Tourism](https://karnatakatourism.org/en/experiences/channapatna-toys/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channapatna_toys)
- Etikoppaka (Andhra Pradesh) makes turned-wood lacquer toys with natural dyes. GI-tag retail listings exist — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etikoppaka); [Indyhaat](https://indyhaat.co.in/product-category/etikoppaka-toys/)
- Labour cost: unskilled and semi-skilled workers in India earn ₹9,000-15,000 a month ($108-180), against China's minimum wage of ¥1,420-2,690 a month ($199-376). Manufacturers say India cannot match China's efficiency yet — [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/a/toy-manufacturers-shift-from-china-not-child-s-play-/7446538.html)
- Playgro (Manu Gupta) and Sunlord (Amitabh Kharbanda) are Indian manufacturers in JV and contract talks with global brands, helped by US tariffs on China and Vietnam — [Snackfax](https://snackfax.com/business/indias-toy-industry-gears-up-for-1-billion-breakout-as-us-tariffs-hit-china-54-and-vietnam-46-playgros-manu-gupta-eyes-global-jv-surge/)
- Indian toy exports reach 153 countries — [Organiser, Jul 2025](https://organiser.org/2025/07/08/301630/bharat/atmanirbhar-bharat-and-its-toy-story-how-we-went-from-importing-to-exporting-to-153-countries-in-just-8-years/)

### Inferences
- A practical route for a non-toxic D2C brand:
  - Wooden and Montessori SKUs from Channapatna or Etikoppaka artisans, or organised Indian wooden-toy units, provided they hold BIS IS 9873.
  - Plastic and STEM SKUs from BIS-licensed contract manufacturers in NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru or Koppal.
  - Use China only for BIS-exempt inputs or components where legally allowed.
- Artisan clusters are unlikely to hold BIS licences individually. Aggregators or cooperatives with a licence, or the brand's own licensed finishing unit, may be needed. This is not verified.

### Gaps
- Verified 2025-26 wholesale prices for Channapatna and Kondapalli toys, contract-manufacturing quotes from Indian OEMs (price per unit, MOQs), and a published India-vs-China unit-cost premium (for example "10-15% higher") were not found.
- Details on Kondapalli and on the Delhi-NCR and Mumbai toy clusters were not retrieved.
