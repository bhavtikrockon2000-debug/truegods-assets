## 2. Recurring praise and complaints

### Takeaway
Praise centres on value for money, sparkle and craftsmanship ("looks better in person") and attentive, multi-channel communication. The most serious complaints are about **production and "crafting" delays against promised dates**, including one case where paid expedited shipping missed a proposal. That case also includes an allegation that the company offered a gift in exchange for changing the review.

### Cited Findings
- **Praise, quality:** rings often "look even better in person than in provided photos"; reviewers call them sturdy, luxurious and well made with careful detail. They also mention secure stone settings, comfortable wear and strong sparkle — [SmartCustomer summary](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/caratbee.com); [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com).
- **Praise, value:** reviewers say the balance of quality and price is good — [SmartCustomer](https://www.smartcustomer.com/reviews/caratbee.com).
- **Praise, service:** CaratBee "keeps in touch every step of the way" and is reachable by phone, text or email. Reviewers call staff responsive and attentive, like the custom-design options and wide stone choice, and report "quick shipping and careful packaging" — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com); [ScamAdviser summary](https://www.scamadviser.com/check-website/caratbee.com).
- **Praise, exchanges:** RealReviews snippets mention "responsive service including easy exchanges" — [RealReviews](https://realreviews.io/reviews/caratbee.com).
- **Complaint, delays and dishonesty (most serious found):** a Trustpilot reviewer described a "total failure in both logistics and basic honesty." They paid for expedited shipping on an engagement ring for a proposal, but the ring "sat in 'Crafting' for 10 days with zero communication," and they said they were "repeatedly fed lies and given empty promises about completion dates" — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com).
- **Complaint, review manipulation allegation:** one Trustpilot reviewer said CaratBee **offered earrings on condition that they modify or remove their review**, and criticised poor communication as their engagement deadline slipped. The reviewer later **raised the rating from 1 to 3 stars** after CaratBee sped up the package, refunded the expedited shipping, **paid the import duties** and the ring "looked phenomenal" — search summary of [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com) / [Trustpilot CA p.2](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com?page=2). The snippets do not make clear whether this is the same reviewer as the "total failure" review.
- **Company response pattern:** CaratBee replies publicly on Trustpilot. It acknowledged "a small delay during the crafting stage" and refunded the express shipping fee — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com).
- Other reviewers mention "hiccups in shipping timelines", with one saying the quality made up for the delay — [Trustpilot CA p.2](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com?page=2).
- **Policy friction (from the brand's own policies):** returns within 30 days; **limit of two returns or exchanges per customer per year**; refunds go only to the original payment method — [Caratbee FAQ](https://caratbee.com/pages/faqs), [Refund policy](https://caratbee.com/policies/refund-policy). The refund policy reportedly **excludes wrong-size orders** from returns — [Refund policy](https://caratbee.com/policies/refund-policy) (search summary).
- **Shipping safeguards:** transit insurance and signature on delivery — [Caratbee FAQ](https://caratbee.com/pages/faqs).

### Inferences
- The main operational weakness is **made-to-order lead time and date reliability**, not product quality. For engagement rings, the proposal date is the product. A competitor offering guaranteed ship dates (or ready-to-ship stock held in the US) with proactive status updates would address the most damaging complaint type directly.
- Caratbee's service recovery is generous: it refunded express fees, covered duties and rushed the ring. But recovery can also mean offering incentives around reviews. On Trustpilot, offering an incentive to change a review breaks platform rules, and in the US it falls under the FTC's 2024 fake-reviews rule. This is a reputational and regulatory exposure, and a trust gap a competitor can exploit with clean, transparent review practices.
- The two-returns-per-year cap and the wrong-size exclusion are unusually restrictive against US norms (typically 30–100-day returns, free first resize). A newcomer can differentiate with a free resize and a simple US return address.

### Gaps
- There is no systematic breakdown of negative reviews by theme or date; only a handful of complaint snippets surfaced.
- Complaints about stone certification accuracy (whether IGI reports match the stones) were not found. This may reflect limited data rather than a clean record.
- Specific feedback on resizing and packaging (beyond "careful packaging") was not found.

## 4. US tariffs, the end of de minimis, and customer-paid duties

### Takeaway
Caratbee's FAQ puts duties and taxes on the customer ("courier will contact you to collect"). In at least one documented dispute, Caratbee paid the import duties as a goodwill gesture. No customer reviews specifically about post-2025 tariff surprises were found.

### Cited Findings
- FAQ: customers "may be required to pay duties and taxes upon the package's arrival," the courier contacts the customer to collect them, and the amount "depends on your state or country government" — [Caratbee FAQ](https://caratbee.com/pages/faqs) (search summary).
- One Trustpilot case: CaratBee "paid duty import costs" as part of resolving a delayed-ring complaint — [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/caratbee.com).
- A search summary said the US $800 de minimis exemption is suspended for all shipments, citing a "June 24, 2026 CBP rule". This claim came from an AI search summary with no identifiable primary source and is **unverified**; it conflicts with the commonly reported August 29, 2025 suspension date. The writer should rely on the tariff-policy researcher's sourced notes instead.

### Inferences
- A delivered-duty-unpaid (DDU) model means US buyers of India-made jewellery can face an unexpected courier bill at the door, on top of US reciprocal tariffs on Indian goods. That is a clear friction and trust gap. A competitor offering **DDP (duties paid, "no surprise fees at delivery")**, or shipping from US stock, can market directly against it.
- Caratbee covering duties only as service recovery suggests duties are already a pain point that surfaces in escalations.

### Gaps
- No review explicitly mentions 2025–2026 tariff surcharges, checkout "tariff fees" or refused deliveries because of duties. The snippets did not expose review dates, so the post-tariff trend cannot be measured.
- Whether Caratbee has since switched to DDP or added tariff surcharges at checkout (a 2025–2026 policy change) could not be confirmed because caratbee.com was blocked.
